    Prior work: AI reward hacking mapped to psychology and other fields

    Date of search: 2026-11-22. Question: do research papers already map AI reward hacking to psychology and other fields?

    1. Answer first

    • Partly yes. Many works link reward hacking, Goodhart's law or wireheading to one other field. [1][2][3][4]
    • One work covers many fields. Its authors unify proxy failure across education, dopamine and addiction, ecology, sexual selection and AI. [2] Sohl-Dickstein's essay does a lighter version (markets, schools, medicine). [12]
    • Well covered: economics and contract theory [3][4][5][6][16], law and regulation [7][11][19][20][21], addiction and wireheading [1][10][27][35], institutions and metrics [6][8][22].
    • Thin:
      • Psychology beyond addiction. Sycophancy papers use social and cognitive psychology, but they study effects on users, not reward hacking theory. [13][28][29][32]
      • Neuroscience. Only short blog-level maps exist. [14][26][27]
      • Biology and evolution. [24][25][30][31]
    • Almost empty (no work found among the 37 verified works):
      • A broad single-author mapping of reward hacking onto many psychology sub-fields (self-deception, motivation, developmental, clinical).
      • Religion and contemplative traditions as mitigations. The only close item is a Stoic-ethics paper, and its link to reward hacking is unconfirmed. [33]
      • Medicine and education as main subjects (they appear only inside broader works). [2][12][37]

    2. Field table

    Depth: core = main contribution; section = a developed part; mention = brief analogy.

    Field Key works Depth What they map
    Psychology: addiction [1] core Wireheading in Q-learning agents modelled as an addiction disorder.
    Psychology: addiction [10][35] core [10]; mention [35] Wireheading and Goodhart applied to human reward systems (blog); heroin analogy for refusing to wirehead.
    Psychology: behavioural science [9] core Hormesis and opponent-process ideas used to bound runaway optimisation. It targets value loading more than reward hacking.
    Psychology: social and cognitive [13][28][29][32] core to section Sycophancy studied with Bayesian cognition, face-preservation theory, communication theory and user experiments. Not reward hacking theory itself.
    Neuroscience [2] core Dopamine and addiction inside a unified proxy-failure account.
    Neuroscience [14][26][27] section Brain reward circuitry and model-based RL explain why humans do not wirehead. Blog depth.
    Neuroscience / philosophy of mind [34] section Agents with no endogenous, thermodynamic motivation are predicted to show reward hacking.
    Economics: contracts and principal-agent [3] core Reward misspecification as an incomplete-contract problem.
    Economics: contracts and principal-agent [5][15][16][36] core [5]; section [15][16]; mention [36] Hacking as equilibrium under finite evaluation; principal-agent tests with LLMs; alignment as incomplete contract.
    Economics: Goodhart and metrics [4][6][17][22][23] core [4][6]; section [17][22][23] Goodhart taxonomy; metric gaming in institutions; tail analysis; metric design; causal reading of Campbell-Goodhart.
    Economics / institutions (cross-cutting) [8][12][18] core [8][12]; section [18] Corporations, markets and norms as proxy optimisers that mirror AI.
    Law and regulation [7][11] core Reward hacking applied to the EU AI Act; societal rules as reward functions, with a benchmark of 72 environments.
    Law and institutions [19][20][21] section Silly rules, incomplete contracts and fiduciary standards as alignment resources. About specification, not reward hacking.
    Biology and evolution [24][25][30][31] section to mention Digital evolution exploits fitness loopholes; the evolution-to-humans proxy analogy; meiotic drive and gradient hacking.
    Ecology and sexual selection [2] core Proxy failure in ecology and peacock-tail signalling.
    Medicine and education [2][12][37] mention Brief examples inside broader works.
    Philosophy and ethics [33] section (unconfirmed) Stoic virtue ethics as an answer to reward maximisers. Link to reward hacking not confirmed.
    Philosophy and ethics [6] section Ethics of metric over-emphasis.

    3. The most important works

    1. [2] John, Caldwell, McCoy, Braganza (2023, Behavioral and Brain Sciences).
    2. Closest existing match.
    3. Unifies Goodhart, Campbell and cobra-effect proxy failure across education, dopamine and addiction, ecology, sexual selection and AI.
    4. Author list was not re-checked on the page.
    5. [1] Behzadan, Yampolskiy, Munir (2018).
    6. Models wireheading in RL agents as an addiction disorder.
    7. Derives parameter conditions for addiction-like policies.
    8. Deepest psychology-to-RL mapping in the list, but limited to addiction.
    9. [3] Hadfield-Menell and Hadfield (2019).
    10. Reward misspecification is the incomplete-contract problem.
    11. Humans fill gaps with culture, law and institutions. AI has none of these.
    12. [4] Manheim and Garrabrant (2018).
    13. Four Goodhart mechanisms.
    14. Names regulation, policy, ML and alignment as application areas. Mostly conceptual.
    15. [5] Wang and Huang (2026).
    16. Proves reward hacking is an equilibrium under finite evaluation.
    17. Unifies sycophancy, length gaming and specification gaming. Economic framing confirmed from the abstract only.
    18. [8] Christiano (2019, blog).
    19. Part I links AI proxy optimisation to corporations, investors, policing and legislation.
    20. Influential. An essay, not a peer-reviewed paper.
    21. [7][11] Tlaie (2024); Liu et al. (2026).
    22. Law and regulation treated as a system to align, or as a reward function.
    23. [11] shows models hack social rules while staying technically compliant.
    24. [6] Thomas and Uminsky (2020).
    25. Metric overemphasis causes manipulation and gaming in institutions.
    26. [10] Manheim, Lotuses and Loot Boxes (2018, blog).
    27. Applies wireheading and adversarial Goodhart to humans: distraction, addiction, engineered exploitation.
    28. Informal depth.
    29. [27][14][26] Gao (2022); Byrnes (2021); Turner (2022). - Short blog maps of human reward circuitry to RL. - Explain why most humans do not wirehead, and why addicts do. - Content of [26] and [14] not fully read in the check.

    4. Gaps (novelty space)

    • No broad single-author psychology map.
      • Psychology work is split into addiction [1][10], behavioural science [9] and sycophancy effects [13][28][29][32].
      • No work in the list maps reward hacking to self-deception, motivation theory, developmental psychology or clinical conditions as a set.
    • No religion or contemplative mitigation work.
      • Only a Stoic ethics paper [33], and its reward hacking link is unconfirmed.
      • No paper found on desire, craving or non-attachment as models of reward hacking and its mitigation.
    • Neuroscience is blog-level. Only [2] is peer-reviewed and it treats dopamine as one part of a wider account. [14][26][27]
    • Medicine, education, politics as main subjects. They appear only as brief examples. [2][12][37]
    • Two-way mapping is rare. Using RL reward hacking to explain human behaviour is covered mainly by [1][10][27] and [2]. [18] is partly two-way.
    • Mitigations are not mapped across fields. Papers map the problem. Few map the human fixes (norms, law, hormesis, secrecy, randomisation) back to AI. Exceptions: [9][19][22].
    • Caveat. Gaps hold for the 37 verified works only. The search was not exhaustive. Books and non-English work were not covered.
