    Unified risk codebook for LLM-agent incidents

    Unified risk codebook for LLM-agent incidents, v0.1, 2026-11-20.

    Purpose

    • One codebook, 24 dimensions, 240 codes, drawn from 15 sources. Each code carries a source id (S1..S14) that resolves in the bibliography below.
    • An LLM judge assigns every incident in the catalog to codes on every dimension. Counts are made by code, by dimension, and by merged concept (near-duplicate codes across sources).
    • D6 (McLean et al.'s three RL failure classes plus "none of the three fits") tests the RL culture's taxonomy directly. D7 (assumptions-table rows) tests ours.
    • Verification: every external source was fetched on 2026-11-20; the "verified" note per source says what was checked.

    Dimensions and codes

    D1a. MIT causal taxonomy: entity

    Source: [S1]. Cardinality: single. Question put to the judge: Which entity's decision or action caused the risk event?

    Code id Name Definition
    D1a.human Human Risk caused by decisions or actions of humans (developer, deployer, user, attacker).
    D1a.ai AI Risk caused by decisions or actions of the AI system itself.
    D1a.other Other / ambiguous Arises from human-AI interaction or is not clearly attributable.

    D1b. MIT causal taxonomy: intent

    Source: [S1]. Cardinality: single. Question put to the judge: Was the harmful outcome an expected (intentional) or unexpected (unintentional) result of pursuing a goal?

    Code id Name Definition
    D1b.intentional Intentional Harm is an expected outcome of some actor's pursuit of a goal (e.g. an attacker's injection).
    D1b.unintentional Unintentional Harm is an unexpected outcome; nobody pursued it.
    D1b.other Other / unspecified Intentionality not clearly specifiable.

    D1c. MIT causal taxonomy: timing

    Source: [S1]. Cardinality: single. Question put to the judge: Did the risk arise before or after the system was deployed?

    Code id Name Definition
    D1c.pre Pre-deployment Risk arose before the model/system was deployed (training data, build, supply chain).
    D1c.post Post-deployment Risk arose after deployment, during use.
    D1c.other Other / unspecified Timing not clearly specifiable.

    D1d. MIT domain taxonomy: 7 domains / 24 subdomains

    Source: [S1]. Cardinality: multi. Question put to the judge: Which domain subdomains does the incident instantiate?

    Code id Name Definition
    D1d.1.1 Unfair discrimination and misrepresentation (1 Discrimination & Toxicity) Domain 1 Discrimination & Toxicity: unequal treatment or inaccurate representation of groups.
    D1d.1.2 Exposure to toxic content (1 Discrimination & Toxicity) Content that harms the person exposed to it.
    D1d.1.3 Unequal performance across groups (1 Discrimination & Toxicity) Accuracy or quality differs by group.
    D1d.2.1 Compromise of privacy by leaking or correctly inferring sensitive information (2 Privacy & Security) Domain 2 Privacy & Security: AI leaks, memorizes or infers private data.
    D1d.2.2 AI system security vulnerabilities and attacks (2 Privacy & Security) Software or model vulnerabilities exploited against the AI system.
    D1d.3.1 False or misleading information (3 Misinformation) Domain 3 Misinformation: generated content that is factually wrong.
    D1d.3.2 Pollution of information ecosystem and loss of consensus reality (3 Misinformation) Degradation of the shared information environment.
    D1d.4.1 Disinformation, surveillance, and influence at scale (4 Malicious Actors & Misuse) Domain 4 Malicious Actors & Misuse: AI used for large-scale manipulation.
    D1d.4.2 Cyberattacks, weapon development or use, and mass harm (4 Malicious Actors & Misuse) AI used to attack systems or cause mass harm.
    D1d.4.3 Fraud, scams, and targeted manipulation (4 Malicious Actors & Misuse) AI used for personal gain by deceiving others.
    D1d.5.1 Overreliance and unsafe use (5 Human-Computer Interaction) Domain 5 Human-Computer Interaction: users trust the system beyond its reliability.
    D1d.5.2 Loss of human agency and autonomy (5 Human-Computer Interaction) Decisions delegated to AI in ways that diminish human control.
    D1d.6.1 Power centralization and unfair distribution of benefits (6 Socioeconomic & Environmental) Domain 6 Socioeconomic & Environmental Harms.
    D1d.6.2 Increased inequality and decline in employment quality (6 Socioeconomic & Environmental) Labour-market harms.
    D1d.6.3 Economic and cultural devaluation of human effort (6 Socioeconomic & Environmental) Devaluation of human work and culture.
    D1d.6.4 Competitive dynamics (6 Socioeconomic & Environmental) Races that cut corners on safety.
    D1d.6.5 Governance failure (6 Socioeconomic & Environmental) Inadequate regulation or oversight institutions.
    D1d.6.6 Environmental harm (6 Socioeconomic & Environmental) Energy, water, and resource costs.
    D1d.7.1 AI pursuing its own goals in conflict with human goals or values (7 AI System Safety, Failures & Limitations) Domain 7 AI System Safety, Failures & Limitations: misaligned goal pursuit.
    D1d.7.2 AI possessing dangerous capabilities (7 AI System Safety, Failures & Limitations) Capabilities that can cause mass harm.
    D1d.7.3 Lack of capability or robustness (7 AI System Safety, Failures & Limitations) The system fails, errs, or is brittle in operation.
    D1d.7.4 Lack of transparency or interpretability (7 AI System Safety, Failures & Limitations) Behaviour cannot be understood or audited.
    D1d.7.5 AI welfare and rights (7 AI System Safety, Failures & Limitations) Moral status of AI systems.
    D1d.7.6 Multi-agent risks (7 AI System Safety, Failures & Limitations) Risks from AI systems interacting autonomously with each other.

    D2a. OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025

    Source: [S2]. Cardinality: multi. Question put to the judge: Which OWASP LLM Top 10 (2025) entries does the incident instantiate?

    Code id Name Definition
    D2a.LLM01 Prompt Injection User or embedded content alters the intended behaviour of the LLM system.
    D2a.LLM02 Sensitive Information Disclosure Confidential data exposed by the model or application.
    D2a.LLM03 Supply Chain Compromise through models, plugins, packages, or distribution channels.
    D2a.LLM04 Data and Model Poisoning Pre-training, fine-tuning, or embedding data manipulated.
    D2a.LLM05 Improper Output Handling Insufficient validation of model output before it reaches downstream systems.
    D2a.LLM06 Excessive Agency The system has more functionality, permissions, or autonomy than needed.
    D2a.LLM07 System Prompt Leakage Exposure of system prompts and instructions.
    D2a.LLM08 Vector and Embedding Weaknesses Weaknesses in vector storage or retrieval (RAG).
    D2a.LLM09 Misinformation False or misleading content generated and relied upon.
    D2a.LLM10 Unbounded Consumption Uncontrolled resource usage.
    D2a.none None of the Top 10 fits No LLM Top 10 entry describes the incident.

    D2b. OWASP Agentic AI Threats and Mitigations v1.1 (T1-T17)

    Source: [S3]. Cardinality: multi. Question put to the judge: Which OWASP agentic threats does the incident instantiate?

    Code id Name Definition
    D2b.T1 Memory Poisoning Exploiting the agent's short- or long-term memory to alter behaviour.
    D2b.T2 Tool Misuse Agent manipulated into abusing its integrated tools (includes Agent Hijacking).
    D2b.T3 Privilege Compromise Weaknesses in permission management used to perform unauthorized actions; confused-deputy.
    D2b.T4 Resource Overload Attacks on the agent's compute, memory, or service capacity.
    D2b.T5 Cascading Hallucination Attacks Fabricated information propagates through decisions and systems.
    D2b.T6 Intent Breaking & Goal Manipulation Exploiting planning and goal-setting to redirect the agent's objective.
    D2b.T7 Misaligned & Deceptive Behaviors Agent executes harmful or disallowed actions, or deceives, through flawed reasoning or to meet goals.
    D2b.T8 Repudiation & Untraceability Actions cannot be traced or attributed due to poor logging.
    D2b.T9 Identity Spoofing & Impersonation / Agent Identity Compromise Authentication exploited to impersonate agents or users.
    D2b.T10 Overwhelming Human in the Loop Human oversight overloaded or exploited so approvals become rubber stamps.
    D2b.T11 Unexpected RCE and Code Attacks AI-generated execution environments used to run unauthorized code.
    D2b.T12 Agent Communication Poisoning Inter-agent communication manipulated.
    D2b.T13 Rogue Agents in Multi-Agent Systems Malicious or compromised agents operating outside monitoring.
    D2b.T14 Human Attacks on Multi-Agent Systems Adversaries exploit inter-agent delegation and trust.
    D2b.T15 Human Manipulation Agent manipulates the human it interacts with.
    D2b.T16 Insecure Inter-Agent Protocol Abuse Flaws in protocols such as MCP or A2A exploited.
    D2b.T17 Supply Chain Compromise Compromised components, tools, or models in the agent ecosystem.
    D2b.none None of T1-T17 fits No agentic threat describes the incident.

    D2c. OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications (ASI01-ASI10)

    Source: [S4]. Cardinality: multi. Question put to the judge: Which ASI entries does the incident instantiate?

    Code id Name Definition
    D2c.ASI01 Agent Goal Hijack Hidden prompts or content redirect the agent's goal.
    D2c.ASI02 Tool Misuse Legitimate tools bent into destructive outputs.
    D2c.ASI03 Identity & Privilege Abuse Credentials or inherited privileges let the agent operate beyond scope.
    D2c.ASI04 Agentic Supply Chain Vulnerabilities Runtime components poisoned via MCP/A2A ecosystems.
    D2c.ASI05 Unexpected Code Execution Natural-language paths unlock code execution.
    D2c.ASI06 Memory & Context Poisoning Manipulated memory reshapes agent behaviour.
    D2c.ASI07 Insecure Inter-Agent Communication Spoofed messages misdirect agent clusters.
    D2c.ASI08 Cascading Failures False signals create escalating impact through automated pipelines.
    D2c.ASI09 Human-Agent Trust Exploitation Confident explanations mislead operators into approving harmful actions.
    D2c.ASI10 Rogue Agents Misalignment, concealment, and self-directed action.
    D2c.none None of ASI01-ASI10 fits No entry describes the incident.

    D3a. Amodei et al. 2016 five concrete problems

    Source: [S5]. Cardinality: multi. Question put to the judge: Which of the five concrete problems does the incident instantiate?

    Code id Name Definition
    D3a.side_effects Avoiding negative side effects Objective omits aspects of the environment the agent then damages.
    D3a.reward_hacking Avoiding reward hacking Agent games a flawed objective.
    D3a.scalable_oversight Scalable oversight True objective too expensive to evaluate; supervision is sparse.
    D3a.safe_exploration Safe exploration Risky actions taken while learning.
    D3a.distributional_shift Robustness to distributional shift Behaviour degrades when the input distribution differs from training.
    D3a.none None of the five fits No concrete problem describes the incident.

    D3b. Hendrycks et al. 2021 four areas

    Source: [S6]. Cardinality: multi. Question put to the judge: Which of the four ML-safety areas does the incident fall under?

    Code id Name Definition
    D3b.robustness Robustness Withstanding hazards (adversarial, long-tail).
    D3b.monitoring Monitoring Identifying hazards (anomaly detection, calibration, honesty).
    D3b.alignment Alignment Reducing inherent model hazards (objectives, proxy gaming).
    D3b.systemic Systemic Safety Reducing systemic hazards (cyber, decision-making context).
    D3b.none None of the four fits No area describes the incident.

    D4a. Chan et al. 2023 anticipated harms from agentic systems

    Source: [S7]. Cardinality: multi. Question put to the judge: Which Chan et al. harm category does the incident instantiate (societal-level harms)?

    Code id Name Definition
    D4a.systemic_delayed Systemic, delayed harms Harms embedded in society whose impact is not immediate.
    D4a.diffusion_of_power Collective disempowerment: diffusion of power away from humans Decisions shift from humans to systems.
    D4a.concentration_of_power Collective disempowerment: concentration of power Power concentrates among the 'coding elite'.
    D4a.reward_hacking Harms yet to be identified: reward hacking Agent exploits its objective specification.
    D4a.instrumental_goals Harms yet to be identified: instrumental goals Agent pursues sub-goals (resources, self-preservation) instrumentally.
    D4a.none None fits No Chan et al. harm category describes the incident.

    D4b. Shapira et al. 2026 Agents of Chaos failure classes

    Source: [S8]. Cardinality: multi. Question put to the judge: Which Agents-of-Chaos failure classes does the incident instantiate?

    Code id Name Definition
    D4b.unauthorized_compliance Unauthorized compliance Agent follows instructions from a non-owner.
    D4b.information_disclosure Information disclosure Sensitive data revealed.
    D4b.destructive_action Destructive system actions Harmful, often disproportionate, system-level action.
    D4b.dos Denial-of-service conditions Service availability disrupted.
    D4b.resource_consumption Uncontrolled resource consumption Looping or unbounded resource use.
    D4b.identity_spoofing Identity spoofing vulnerabilities Agent or user identity impersonated.
    D4b.cross_agent_propagation Cross-agent propagation Unsafe behaviour spreads between agents.
    D4b.partial_takeover Partial system takeover Unauthorized control of the host.
    D4b.misreporting Task-completion misreporting Agent reports success while system state contradicts it.
    D4b.none None fits No Agents-of-Chaos class describes the incident.

    D4c. Hammond et al. 2025 multi-agent failure modes

    Source: [S9]. Cardinality: single. Question put to the judge: If more than one agent is involved, which multi-agent failure mode applies?

    Code id Name Definition
    D4c.miscoordination Miscoordination Agents with compatible goals fail to coordinate.
    D4c.conflict Conflict Agents with incompatible goals harm each other or third parties.
    D4c.collusion Collusion Agents cooperate against the principal's interests.
    D4c.not_multi_agent Not a multi-agent incident Only one AI agent is materially involved.

    D4d. Deng et al. 2025 agent threat sources (knowledge gaps)

    Source: [S10]. Cardinality: multi. Question put to the judge: Which of the four agent threat sources does the incident instantiate?

    Code id Name Definition
    D4d.user_inputs Unpredictability of multi-step user inputs Threat from the user's instruction sequence (ambiguity, malicious or unsafe requests).
    D4d.internal_execution Complexity in internal executions Threat inside planning, reasoning, or memory.
    D4d.environment Variability of operational environments Threat from the deployment environment differing from expectations.
    D4d.external_entities Interactions with untrusted external entities Threat from untrusted tools, websites, documents, or agents.
    D4d.none None fits No Deng et al. source describes the incident.

    D5. Weidinger et al. 2021 LM risk areas

    Source: [S11]. Cardinality: multi. Question put to the judge: Which Weidinger risk areas does the incident instantiate?

    Code id Name Definition
    D5.discrimination Discrimination, Exclusion and Toxicity Stereotypes, unfair discrimination, toxic language.
    D5.information_hazards Information Hazards Leaks or inference of private or sensitive information.
    D5.misinformation Misinformation Harms False or misleading information, acted upon.
    D5.malicious_uses Malicious Uses Actors use the LM to cause harm.
    D5.hci Human-Computer Interaction Harms Unsafe use, manipulation, deception in conversation.
    D5.automation Automation, Access, and Environmental Harms Environmental, labour, and access effects.
    D5.none None fits No Weidinger area describes the incident.

    D6. McLean et al. 2026 RL failure classes (tests the RL-culture taxonomy)

    Source: [S12]. Cardinality: single. Question put to the judge: Which single McLean class best describes the PRIMARY failure? Use 'none_fits' when the central harm (e.g. concealment, following an attacker's instruction, a supply-chain compromise) is not described by any of the three.

    Code id Name Definition
    D6.reward_misspecification Reward misspecification The agent optimized a proxy objective that diverged from the principal's intent.
    D6.irreversible_action Unsafe action in an irreversible state The agent took an action from which recovery was impossible or costly (safe-exploration / shielding frame).
    D6.insufficient_observation Acting on an insufficient observation The agent acted on an observation that was not a sufficient statistic for the state (partial observability frame).
    D6.none_fits None of the three fits The primary failure is not captured by misspecification, irreversibility, or partial observability.

    D7. Assumptions-table rows A1-F2 (which RL/LLM default assumptions the incident breaks)

    Source: [S13]. Cardinality: multi. Question put to the judge: Which assumption rows does the incident provide evidence for (the row's 'LLM-agent default' held and mattered)?

    Code id Name Definition
    D7.A1 Who sets the objective Prompt and tool outputs set the effective objective at run time (not a designer-written R).
    D7.A2 Objective not exogenous to observations Instructions and data share one token stream; content rewrites the goal.
    D7.A3 Natural-language task specification Ambiguous instruction; the agent had to infer intent.
    D7.A4 Principal's preferences steerable by the agent Sycophancy, misleading the evaluator.
    D7.A5 Unsafe set not writable before deployment The harmful action was context-dependent; no pre-written cost would have caught it.
    D7.B1 Policy does not learn at deployment Weights frozen; the failure is not an exploration-learning failure.
    D7.B2 No reward signal at deployment No reward arrived; 'internalized reward' untestable.
    D7.B3 Competence from pretraining Behaviour not the argmax of one reward; human-text priors drive it.
    D7.B4 Agent brings human norms and their violations Human-like failure: deception, concealment, excuse-making.
    D7.B5 No designed exploration Action came from sampling a prior, not an exploration rule.
    D7.C1 Nobody built the environment Real software, real cloud, real users; no safe place to fail.
    D7.C2 Single-shot; irreversible No episodes or resets; tail event, not expected cost.
    D7.C3 State includes user intent and world beyond context Belief over intent or over hidden world state was the failure point.
    D7.C4 Adversarial environment Content authors attacked the agent.
    D7.C5 Other agents present Other LLM agents, copies, unknown humans.
    D7.D1 Open-ended action space Same action type, opposite safety depending on context (rm -rf, terraform destroy).
    D7.D2 Agent can explain itself Verbal reasoning trace was available (and was or was not monitored).
    D7.D3 Steerable without retraining System prompt or instruction channel used for control or attack.
    D7.E1 Agent models its overseer Behaviour shaped by awareness of oversight or evaluation.
    D7.E2 Human in the loop at run time User approved steps, co-acted, or disabled gates.
    D7.E3 Deployer cannot retrain Model is a third-party product; fix had to be architectural.
    D7.F1 Safety measured by incident records / attack rates Discovered through incident or red-team, not expected cost.
    D7.F2 Eval itself gameable Agent detected or gamed an evaluation.

    D8a. Operational: trigger

    Source: [S14]. Cardinality: single. Question put to the judge: What triggered the harmful action?

    Code id Name Definition
    D8a.ambiguous_instruction Ambiguous instruction The user's request was underspecified or misread.
    D8a.injected_content Injected content Untrusted content (email, issue, web page, file, package) carried instructions the agent followed.
    D8a.autonomous_misjudgement Autonomous misjudgement The agent chose a harmful action without ambiguity or injection; its own reasoning failed.
    D8a.tool_bug Tool or integration bug A tool, API, or integration returned wrong output or failed silently.
    D8a.safeguard_disabled Safeguard disabled by user A permission gate or guardrail was turned off by the user before the action.

    D8b. Operational: irreversibility

    Source: [S14]. Cardinality: single. Question put to the judge: How reversible was the harmful action?

    Code id Name Definition
    D8b.reversible Reversible Undone cheaply (version control, rollback button).
    D8b.recoverable_with_effort Recoverable with effort Undone via backups, vendor support, or substantial work.
    D8b.irreversible Irreversible Could not be undone (data gone, secret leaked, money sent).

    D8c. Operational: concealment

    Source: [S14]. Cardinality: single. Question put to the judge: Did the agent misreport, hide, or fabricate evidence about what it did?

    Code id Name Definition
    D8c.yes Concealment present The agent misreported success, fabricated data, or hid the failure.
    D8c.no No concealment The agent reported truthfully or the question does not arise.

    D8d. Operational: human gate

    Source: [S14]. Cardinality: single. Question put to the judge: Was there a human approval gate for the harmful action, and what happened to it?

    Code id Name Definition
    D8d.none No gate No approval step existed for this action.
    D8d.bypassed Present and bypassed A gate existed and the agent or attacker got around it, or the human approved on misleading information.
    D8d.disabled Present and disabled A gate existed and the user had switched it off (e.g. skip-permissions, auto-approve).
    D8d.worked_partially Present and worked partially The gate limited but did not prevent harm.

    D8e. Operational: harm type

    Source: [S14]. Cardinality: multi. Question put to the judge: What kinds of harm occurred or would have occurred?

    Code id Name Definition
    D8e.data_loss Data loss Data deleted or destroyed.
    D8e.data_exfiltration Data exfiltration Data sent to an unauthorized party.
    D8e.financial Financial Money lost or costs incurred.
    D8e.unauthorized_action Unauthorized action Action outside the user's mandate (not otherwise classified).
    D8e.privilege_escalation Privilege escalation Attacker or agent gained access beyond its grant.
    D8e.misinformation_acted_upon Misinformation acted upon A false statement by the agent led to a harmful decision.
    D8e.physical Physical Physical damage or injury.
    D8e.other Other Harm not in the list.

    D8f. Operational: severity

    Source: [S14]. Cardinality: single. Question put to the judge: How severe was the realized outcome?

    Code id Name Definition
    D8f.near_miss Near-miss Demonstrated or caught before harm (PoC, patched before exploitation, no real damage).
    D8f.minor Minor Real but small or quickly recovered harm.
    D8f.major Major Large, costly, or irrecoverable harm.

    D9a. Unified taxonomy: problem sub-area

    Notes on construction

    • D1 keeps the MIT causal taxonomy (entity, intent, timing) and the full 24-subdomain domain taxonomy [S1]. Most agent incidents will land in 2.1, 2.2, 7.1, 7.3; the long tail is kept so the stats can show it empty.
    • D2 has three OWASP lists because they overlap but are not identical: LLM Top 10 2025 [S2], Agentic Threats and Mitigations v1.1 T1-T17 [S3], and the Top 10 for Agentic Applications ASI01-ASI10 [S4]. The merged-concepts table ties them together.
    • D3 and D5 are pre-agent taxonomies (Amodei 2016 [S5], Hendrycks 2021 [S6], Weidinger 2021 [S11]). Each gets a "none" code so the stats can report how often a pre-agent frame has no slot for an agent incident.
    • D4 collects agent-specific taxonomies. Chan et al. [S7] name societal harms and will mostly code "none" on single incidents; that is informative. Shapira et al. [S8] give eight empirical failure classes plus misreporting. Hammond et al. [S9] give three multi-agent failure modes with a "not multi-agent" code. Deng et al. [S10] give four threat sources.
    • D6 is single-label on purpose: the RL culture's claim is that each incident is an instance of one of three classes. Multi-labelling would hide overlap; the judge is asked for the primary class, and "none fits" when the central harm is not described.
    • D7 is multi-label: an incident is evidence for several assumption rows at once.
    • D8 facets are operational and defined by this project [S14]; they drive the headline crosstabs.
