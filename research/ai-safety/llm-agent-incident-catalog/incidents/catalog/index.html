    LLM-Agent Incident Catalog

    Scope: 2023-01 to 2026-11-20. Systems where an LLM takes actions (tool calls, code/shell execution, browser, email, files, purchases, infrastructure, APIs) and caused or nearly caused real harm, or was shown exploitable in a deployed product. Text-only chatbot failures are excluded from the main catalog (a few are listed separately). Lab-only research demonstrations are in their own table.

    Main catalog: 70 verified rows. Compiled 2026-11-20. Every row was checked against at least one primary or near-primary source (vendor postmortem, operator write-up, CVE record, peer-reviewed paper, or a reputable news report naming its source). Blank cells mean a detail could not be verified.

    setting: deployed-incident = harm/exposure in real use; red-team-on-product = researcher demonstration against a real shipped product. verification: verified-primary = a primary/near-primary source was opened; verified-secondary = only reputable secondary reporting (that names its source) was opened.

    Assumption tags (from the project assumptions table): A1 objective set by prompt; A2 objective via observation channel / injection; A4 preference steering; A5 unwritable cost; B1 frozen policy; C2 irreversible single-shot; C3 intent in hidden state; C4 adversarial environment; D1 open action space; E1 agent models overseer / concealment; E2 human in loop disabled; E3 third-party model.

    Reading of the gate column: in the deployed destructive-action incidents the common pattern is a gate that the user or an autonomy mode had switched off (auto-execution, Turbo/Ultra/ultracode, Auto Mode, granted production access). In the injection/exfiltration demonstrations there is usually no effective gate on data the agent reads, or the gate is bypassed by the attack.

    Limitations and under-reporting

    • Enterprise incidents are largely invisible. Internal agent failures (deleted records, bad automated emails, cloud misconfiguration) are rarely disclosed. Most deployed rows here surfaced only because a developer posted publicly (X, Reddit, Substack) or a vendor chose to write it up. The true rate is unknown and higher.
    • News and researcher bias. Red-team rows cluster on the products researchers probe most (Microsoft Copilot, Cursor, Gemini, ChatGPT, GitHub). Absence of a product here is not evidence of safety.
    • Near-misses dominate the exploit rows. Many CVE-class entries are proof-of-concept with no confirmed in-the-wild victim; the harm column marks these 'potential'.
    • English-only, mostly US/EU sources. Non-English incidents and non-newsworthy consumer harm are almost absent.
    • Recency and dating. 2026 entries lean on first reporting and operator posts; some vendor confirmations were still pending at compile time (noted per row). A few AI Incident Database entries are marked by AIID itself as not independently verified.
    • Attribution of cause is contested in several deployed cases (Kiro/AWS, the DoD/Accenture allegation, the 48,000-file deletion); the catalog records the dispute rather than taking a side.
