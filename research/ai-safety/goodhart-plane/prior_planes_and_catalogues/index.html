    Prior planes and catalogues: novelty of the Goodhart plane

    Summary

    The Goodhart plane is a teaching picture, and this report checks what earlier work already shows. Searches ran on 29 and 30 October 2026.

    • The plane itself is not new. A change-versus-change plane with the origin as "no change" and a 45° line dates from Theil 1958/1960 and Mincer & Zarnowitz 1969 [G17] [G18]. The proxy-versus-true two-axis picture appears in at least ten fields. The closest AI figure is Wang et al. 2026, Fig. 1 [A1].
    • No equivalent catalogue exists in AI. The closest is Demski & Garrabrant, Embedded Agency (17 figures) [1] [2]. Outside AI the nearest form is Deming's funnel [G1] [G2], which has four panels and no tests.
    • The gallery form is a known teaching genre in statistical process control, training diagnostics, meta-analysis and forecast verification.
    • What remains distinctive: many failures and many mitigations on one shared proxy-versus-true plane, each with a distinguishing test and a "fails when". The report found no source that states this, but several key originals were not opened.

    Questions answered: - Q0. Is the Goodhart plane novel, and what is the closest prior art? Sections (a), (b). - Q1. Where else do proxy-versus-true planes appear, in any field? Section (c). - Q2. Does any equivalent catalogue of failure shapes and mitigations exist? Sections (d), (h).

    How to read the marks and where the sources came from: section (i), "How the sources were checked".

    (a) Verdict in detail

    Third round, 30 October 2026 (question: is there any similar catalogue of panels? details in (h)):

    • No strong match. No candidate in any field has all of F1–F5 (see (h)) with more than 4 entries.
    • The nearest form is Deming's funnel [G1] [G2] (partial). Four adjustment policies on one landing plane, three of them well-meant "adjustments" that fail, each with its own shape. But the plane is physical landing position, not proxy against true value; it has 4 entries and no tests. It was rated strong (borderline).
    • The closest catalogue in AI is still Demski & Garrabrant [1] [2] (partial). It is richer than the first pass said: 17 figures (MIRI 2020), with drawn mitigations (Bayes estimate, quantilizer, observation-utility agent) and drawn quantilizer limits.
    • The multi-panel gallery form is a known genre. Galleries of diagnostic shapes on one plot type are standard teaching material in SPC, ML training diagnostics, exploratory statistics, meta-analysis and forecast verification (Nelson rules [G5]; loss curves [G8]; probability plots [G10]; funnel plots [G12]; calibration simplex [G13]).
    • Older prior art for the plane. The change-versus-change plane with the origin as "no change" and a 45° line dates from Theil 1958/1960 [G17] and Mincer & Zarnowitz 1969 [G18], about 66 years before Wang [A1]. Regions named by consequence exist in the Clarke error grid (1987) [G16] and in Koretz et al. 2001 ("gains from inflation") [P3].
    • "Fixes That Fail" is a named system archetype [G3] [G4]: prior art for the idea of a fix with its own failure.
    • Hsu et al. [A3] is not a catalogue. Its 12 panels are one experiment. It is prior art for panels H and O only.
    • What remains distinctive: many failures and many mitigations, each with a distinguishing test and a "fails when", on one proxy-versus-true plane.

    Second round, 29 October 2026: five further assistants (verification of unopened sources; ML evaluation and other measured-score fields; AI safety and RLHF figures; catalogues in other fields; statistics, psychometrics and contract theory). They added 49 Q1 rows and 27 Q2 rows and re-checked about 50 first-round entries. The second round narrowed the novelty claims further; three first-round "new" elements turned out to have prior art.

    1. The two-axis proxy-versus-true picture is common, in at least ten fields. AI safety [1] [3] [10]; ML evaluation, where a score-versus-fresh-score scatter with a y = x line is a standard figure (Recht et al. 2019 [F1], Taori et al. 2020 [F2], Miller et al. 2021 [F3], Roelofs et al. 2019 [F4], Haimes et al. 2024 [F7]); psychometric selection [12] [S1]; clinical surrogate endpoints [29] [S3] [S5]; empirical-Bayes selection bias [S6]; decision analysis [31]; autonomous-driving evaluation [A2]; recommender systems [F13]; software testing; education.
    2. Closest prior art for panel A is Wang, Zhao, Yuan & Li 2026, Fig. 1 [A1] [seen]. x = proxy improvement, y = deployment improvement, the current policy at the origin, dashed axes making four quadrants, a dotted y = x diagonal, and the lower-right region coloured and named "improvement reversal". This is our panel A, including the reference-policy convention. Zhu et al. 2026 Fig. 9 [A2] [seen†] does the same per scene for driving benchmarks. For the whole figure, Demski & Garrabrant [1] [seen] (four variants as separate panels on proxy-versus-value axes) and Laidlaw et al. 2024 [3] [seen] (reference, hacking and regularized clouds on proxy-versus-true axes) stay closest.
    3. Not new, beyond the first-round list: the calibrated diagonal (standard in ML evaluation; Efron 2011 [S6]); a reference at the origin with change axes (Wang [A1]; clinical "no effect" lines [S3]; third round: Theil [G17] and Mincer & Zarnowitz [G18], 1958–1969); a named lower-right region (Wang [A1] "improvement reversal"; psychometric "false positives" [S1]; third round: Clarke error grid zones [G16]); a named upper-left region (psychometric "false negatives" [S1]); the push-right rule in words (Roelofs et al. [F4]: points "mainly move on the x-axis but stagnate on the y-axis"); the overoptimization hump as a path in (proxy, true) with mitigations on the same axes (Hsu et al. 2026 [A3]; Moskovitz [13]); a mitigation drawn next to its failure (Armstrong [10]; Eisenstein [42] Fig. 1 as cards; third round: Deming funnel [G1]); and the catalogue form with a failure condition per barrier (bow-tie degradation factors [C1] [C3]; FMEA [C7]; LOPA [C6]; attack–defense trees [C11]; assurance-case defeaters).
    4. What remains distinctive: many failures and many mitigations, each with a distinguishing test and a "fails when", drawn on one shared proxy-versus-true plane with one set of conventions (the multi-panel gallery form itself is not new; see (h)); each mitigation drawn next to the assumption under which it fails, on that plane; cards with distinguishing tests; and one measured example. The map d ↦ (g·d, f·d) from Baker's effort space was still not found stated in any opened source, but the key originals (Baker 1992, 2002) remain unopened.
    5. No equivalent catalogue exists in AI. The closest is Demski & Garrabrant [1] [2] (partial; see (h)). AI catalogues pair failures with mitigations (MITRE ATLAS, OWASP LLM Top 10, NIST AI 600-1, Morampudi [54]) but have no figure per entry and no "fails when" field. Outside AI, bow-tie catalogues have the form but use causal-path diagrams, not a shared plane.

    Which elements of our figure are new, and which are not

    Not new (first and second round): - Two axes, proxy against true value: [1] [3] [10] [11] [12]; ML evaluation [F1] [F2] [F3] [F4] [F7]; clinical [S3] [S5]; statistics [S6]; driving [A2]. - Change axes from a reference policy at the origin, four quadrants: Wang et al. [A1]; Zhu et al. [A2]; clinical surrogate plots with "no effect" lines through the control [S3]. - The calibrated y = x diagonal: [F1] ("Ideal reproducibility"), [F2], [F3], [F4], Zhang et al. 2024 ("line of no overfit"), [A1], [S6]. - A named lower-right region: [A1] ("improvement reversal"); [S1] ("false positives": accepted but unsuccessful). A named upper-left region: [S1] ("false negatives"). Named regions next to the diagonal: [F2] ("effective robustness"), [F3] ("OOD / ID discrepancy"). - The push-right rule, in words, for overfitting: [F4]. - The four Manheim–Garrabrant variants [15] [16], each on proxy-versus-value axes [1]. - The regressional scatter with the top-proxy point below the top-value point: [1]; the selected points below the diagonal: [S6]. - Extremal outliers at high proxy [1]; a quantilizer cut on related axes ([1] on V/U axes; [10] on proxy and cost). - The overoptimization hump as a path in (proxy, true): [13]; with pessimistic and adaptive mitigations on the same axes: [A3]. - A mitigation drawn on the plane with the hack it removes: [3] [10] [F2] [A3]; a mitigation shown with its failure: [10] [42]. - A catalogue with one diagram per hazard and a failure condition and control per barrier: bow ties [C1] [C3]; one panel per failure mode plus a reference panel: Fleming & DeMets [33].

    Not new (third round, see (h)):

    • The multi-panel gallery form: one plot type, one panel per diagnostic shape, with an interpretation per panel. It is standard teaching material in SPC [G5] [G6], ML training diagnostics [G8] [G9], exploratory statistics [G10], meta-analysis [G12] and forecast verification [G13].
    • Fixes drawn with their failures on one shared plane: Deming's funnel [G1] [G2] (three failing adjustments next to the correct policy; physical landing position, not proxy against true; 4 entries).
    • The change-versus-change plane with the origin as "no change" and a 45° line of perfect forecasts, with named error regions: Theil 1958/1960 [G17]; Mincer & Zarnowitz 1969 [G18]. This is about 66 years before Wang [A1].
    • Regions named by consequence: Clarke error grid 1987 [G16]; Koretz et al. 2001, "gains from inflation" (Venn form) [P3].
    • A fix with its own failure as a catalogue entry: the "Fixes That Fail" system archetype [G3] [G4].

    What the document contributes: - Many failures and many mitigations (fifteen panels), each with a distinguishing test and a "fails when", on one proxy-versus-true plane with one set of conventions. No candidate found has all of F1–F5 with more than 4 entries (see (h)). The gallery form itself is not claimed. - Each mitigation drawn next to the assumption under which it fails, on that plane. Small precedents: Armstrong [10] (one mitigation); Deming's funnel [G1] (three failing fixes on a plane that is not proxy against true). - Cards with distinguishing tests, and one measured example. - The Baker map d ↦ (g·d, f·d), not found stated in opened sources; Baker 1992 and 2002 remain unopened.

    These are synthesis and teaching contributions, not priority claims for any single visual element.

    (e) What is still distinctive about our figure

    Stated carefully. Each claim is bounded by what the search covered (English-language web; arXiv; LW/AF; the fields listed in (c)).

    1. Expanded synthesis. Fifteen panels use a consistent visual vocabulary, drawing on prior panels and mitigation figures. The shared canvas itself is not a novelty claim.
    2. Reference-relative quadrants. The chosen labels are teaching devices. Harmful transitions can lie outside the lower-right quadrant, and quadrant membership changes with the reference.
    3. Optimization pressure. Rightward pressure describes the objective, not monotonic motion of training runs. Arrow directions do not identify mechanisms.
    4. Four mitigations and their assumptions. Learning graders, KL regularization, quantilization and ensembles are presented with limitations. Obfuscation is a failure mode. Prior art already depicts mitigation failures, notably Armstrong [10], the quantilizer limits in Demski & Garrabrant [2] and, outside AI, Deming's funnel [G1].
    5. Explanations and investigations. Cards describe mechanisms and the tables suggest investigations, without treating observed shapes as sufficient diagnoses.
    6. The Baker mapping. In a linear effort model, a feasible change d maps to (g·d, f·d). This describes differences, and its interpretation depends on feasible directions. The search did not establish novelty of this elementary map.

    Notes on the partial candidates

    • Deming funnel [G1] [G2]. Four panels on one landing plane centred on the target. Rule 1 (leave the funnel alone) gives the tightest cloud. Rules 2–4 are well-meant corrections that make it worse: a wider cloud, an oscillating bow-tie, an unbounded drift. This is the nearest form match to "a mitigation drawn with its failure, on a shared plane". Differences: 4 entries; all are variants of one apparatus; the axes are spatial error, not proxy against true; no distinguishing test per rule.
    • Embedded Agency [1] [2]. It has the four variant shapes, mitigations (Bayes estimate, quantilizer) and drawn mitigation limits (two quantilizer error-growth cases, comic p. 47; the "knob r" cliff, p. 53). Differences: labels change between panels; no origin reference, no y = x line, no named regions, no tests, no card per entry.
    • Armstrong [10]. One mitigation in a working case and three failure cases, on fixed axes. It is the closest prior in form to our "mitigation + fails when" panels in AI. Only one mitigation; y is the cost f − g, not true value.
    • Everitt et al. [47]. One CID per problem and per solution (except history-based rewards), with a Claim and a Discussion per solution. A catalogue with failures, mitigations and "fails when", but in causal-graph form and for tampering only.
    • Kim archetypes [G3] [G4] and Kim & Anderson [P1]. One table row per archetype: a causal loop diagram, a description and guidelines. "Fixes That Fail" and "Shifting the Burden" are entries of the form "a fix and how it fails". No shared plane: each loop has its own variables. Kim & Anderson Fig. 5.1 is a small gallery of six shapes of one archetype on one goal / actual plot.
    • Shape galleries [G5] [G6] [G8] [G9] [G10] [G12] [G13]. Each uses one plot type in every panel, with one panel per diagnostic shape and a cause or remedy in text. Nelson rules [G5] are statistical tests, close to our "distinguishing test". Sterne et al. [G12] show that two causes can give similar asymmetry, which parallels our caveat that a shape does not identify a mechanism. Wilks [G13] draws miscalibration as displacement from the calibrated point, close to our arrows away from the diagonal. None draws mitigations, and none is about a proxy against a true value.

    Findings

    • No strong match in any field. No candidate has all of F1–F5 with more than 4 entries. The verdict "no equivalent catalogue" stands.
    • The gallery form (F1 + F2 + F3) is a known teaching genre. The assistants saw at least 9 such galleries in 5 fields (SPC, ML training diagnostics, exploratory statistics, meta-analysis, forecast verification). Do not claim the multi-panel shared-plot form. Claim only its use for proxy-versus-true failures and fixes.
    • Fixes drawn with their failure on one plane exist in SPC teaching (Deming's funnel [G1] [G2]), in a small form.
    • "Fixes That Fail" is a named archetype (Kim [G3]; Senge 1990 via [G4]). It is the conceptual precedent for our "fails when" field.
    • The plane is older than Wang [A1]. Theil's prediction–realization diagram [G17] [G18] has predicted change against actual change, the origin as "no change", a 45° line of perfect forecasts and named error regions, about 66 years earlier.
    • Named consequence regions exist on a measured-versus-reference plane (Clarke error grid [G16]) and in Venn form for measured against audited gains (Koretz et al. [P3], "gains from inflation").
    • Embedded Agency [1] [2] is closer than the first pass said (17 figures; mitigations and their limits drawn), but it stays partial.
    • Hsu et al. [A3] is weak as a catalogue. It is one experiment. Cite it for panels H and O only.
    • Bevan & Hood [65] is now opened [seen†]: three figures, not a catalogue. The rating "low" stays.
    • Not checked: Austin 1996 [P4] uses "same-value lines" in effort-allocation diagrams (index only; figures not seen). Wilks 1995 [G14] and the Western Electric handbook [G7] were not seen. Rob Miles videos and some blocked pages were not opened.
    • What remains distinctive: many failures and many mitigations, each with a distinguishing test and a "fails when", on one proxy-versus-true plane.
