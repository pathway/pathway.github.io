    Risk vocabulary

    Pitch. A source-grounded glossary of risk, hazard, harm, safety and related terms: 195 verbatim definitions of 102 terms from 38 risk-management, safety-engineering, security and AI-safety sources (ISO 31000, the Society for Risk Analysis glossary, NIST, Leveson, Kaplan and Garrick and others), with a comparison of where the fields conflict and which usage the taxonomy adopts.

    Status: done. Compiled 2026-11-21. Complete for the sources listed; not proofread end to end; not human-reviewed.

    AI use. Most text was generated by Claude (Anthropic) under Robin Chauhan's direction; not fully human-reviewed.

    Limits

    • Paywalled texts were not read: ISO 31000 clause 3 proper, ISO Guide 73, ISO/IEC 23894 and 22989, IEC 60812. ISO wording comes from secondary reproductions (EU Agency for Railways, NIST glossary, Wikipedia).
    • 18 requested items were unavailable. They are listed under "Not obtained" at the end of Definitions by source.
    • Quotes are shortened: any quoted span over 250 characters is cut to its start and marked [...]. Follow the clause and URL for the full text.
    • Some AI-safety definitions come from abstracts only, and a few quotes may differ slightly from the page text. Details are in section 5 of the analysis.

    How to read

    • Every definition is the source's own wording in quotes, with the source number, the clause or section, and a field tag: risk_safety_eng (risk management and safety engineering), security or ai_safety.
    • Source numbers such as [6] are listed in the bibliography, with year, how much of each text was read, and a URL.
    • Definitions by source is the reference: one section per term, one line per definition.
    • The analysis compares the definitions, shows where the fields use one word for different things, and says which usage the AI-risk taxonomy adopts.
    • The interactive explorer shows every source's definition of a term side by side, with search, filters by field and source, and a clickable diagram of the chain below.

    The core chain

    Risk management, safety engineering and security all describe the same chain, with different words at each link.

    risk source / hazard / threat  -->  event (top event, accident)  -->  consequence / harm / loss
        |                                   |                                |
   vulnerability, exposure             likelihood                       severity
                                  risk = f(likelihood, severity)   over the set of scenarios
   controls / barriers / mitigations act on any link
    • Risk source, hazard, threat. The thing with potential to cause harm. "Hazard" is a source in most sources and a system state in Leveson's STPA.
    • Event. What happens: an occurrence or change of circumstances, the top event of a bow-tie, an accident.
    • Consequence, harm, loss. What results. Consequence is neutral, harm is people-centred, loss is relative to what stakeholders value.
    • Risk. Three families of definition are in use: likelihood times severity; the effect of uncertainty on objectives; a set of scenarios with their likelihoods and consequences.
    • Controls. Measures that modify risk, acting on any link.
