    hazard

    • [1, Section 2.2 The Distinction Between Risk and Hazard] (risk_safety_eng) "Hazard, therefore, simply exists as a source. Risk includes the likelihood of conversion of that source into actual delivery of loss, injury, or some form of damage." — Authors quote the dictionary: hazard is 'a source of danger'. Risk is the 'possibility of loss or injury' and the 'degree of probability of such loss.'
    • [1, Section 3.2, footnote 3] (risk_safety_eng) "Having defined risk as a set of triplets, we may now, in line with Section 2.2, define hazard as a set of doublets" — The doublet formula is omitted.
    • [6, 1.9 Hazard] (risk_safety_eng) "A risk source where the potential consequences relate to harm. Hazards could, for example, be associated with energy (e.g., explosion, fire), material (toxic or eco-toxic), biota (pathogens) and information (panic communication)."
    • [7, Chapter 2, Identifying system-level hazards] (risk_safety_eng) "A hazard is a system state or set of conditions that, together with a particular set of worst-case environmental conditions, will lead to a loss." — Leveson hazard differs from most sources: it is a system state, defined relative to the system boundary, not a source of danger.
    • [7, Chapter 3 Integrating STPA, discussion of hazards] (risk_safety_eng) "So for practical reasons, hazards are defined as precursor states to an accident that the system designers never want to occur and thus should be eliminated or controlled in the design process." — Preceded by text rejecting the definition 'a condition that could lead to an accident or a precondition for an accident'.
    • [12, abstract] (ai_safety) "We present four problems ready for research, namely withstanding hazards ("Robustness"), identifying hazards ("Monitoring"), reducing inherent model hazards ("Alignment"), and reducing systemic hazards ("Systemic Safety")." — hazard undefined; inherent model hazards vs systemic hazards
    • [19, Definitions section, p. 3-3] (risk_safety_eng) "An item, event, activity, or situation with the potential to cause: injury, ill-health, or death; damage to or loss of equipment or property; or damage to the environment - (An intermediate state where potential for harm exists)" — Bullets joined.
    • [20, abstract] (ai_safety) "For each category of risk, we describe specific hazards, present illustrative stories, envision ideal scenarios, and propose practical suggestions for mitigating these dangers." — hazard undefined; specific instance within a risk category
    • [26, csrc term 'hazard'; CNSSI 4009-2022 from DoD Dictionary of Military and Associated Terms] (risk_safety_eng) "A condition with the potential to cause injury, illness, or death of personnel; damage to or loss of equipment or property; or mission degradation." — Only one definition on the page. https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/hazard
    • [28, Bowtie element Step 1: Define the Hazard, Definition box] (risk_safety_eng) "A condition, situation, activity or object that could cause or contribute to an aircraft incident or accident." — Aviation-specific wording. URL https://www.caa.co.uk/safety-initiatives/working-with-industry/bowtie/bowtie-elements/hazard/
    • [28, How does bowtie work] (risk_safety_eng) "something in, around or part of an organisation or activity which has the potential to cause damage or harm" — Quoted from the parenthesis in the sentence 'The safety risk picture revolves around the hazard (...)'.     harm

    • [22, Section 1.1, para 1 (context, no stand-alone definition)] (risk_safety_eng) "Negative impact or harm can be experienced by individuals, groups, communities, organizations, society, the environment, and the planet." — AI RMF 1.0 gives no formal definition of 'harm'; this is the closest sentence.
    • [26, csrc term 'harm'; NIST SP 800-122 (privacy sense)] (risk_safety_eng) "Any adverse effects that would be experienced by an individual (i.e., that may be socially, physically, or financially damaging) or an organization if the confidentiality of PII were breached." — Narrow privacy sense, not general harm. https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/harm

    consequence

    • [5, ISO 31000:2018 term Consequence] (risk_safety_eng) "Outcome of an event (3.5) affecting objectives"
    • [6, 1.7 Event, Consequences] (risk_safety_eng) "Consequences: The effects of the activity with respect to the values defined (such as human life and health, environment and economic assets), covering the totality of states, events, barriers and outcomes. The consequences are [...]"
    • [26, csrc term 'consequence'; NIST SP 800-160v1r1 from ISO/IEC 15026-1:2019] (risk_safety_eng) "Effect (change or non-change), usually associated with an event or condition or with the system and usually allowed, facilitated, caused, prevented, changed, or contributed to by the event, condition, or system." — https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/consequence
    • [28, Bowtie element Step 4: Identify Consequences, Definition box] (risk_safety_eng) "A potential event resulting from the release of a Hazard, which directly results in loss or damage." — URL https://www.caa.co.uk/safety-initiatives/working-with-industry/bowtie/bowtie-elements/consequences/ .     impact

    loss

    accident

    incident

    • [26, csrc term 'incident'; FIPS 200] (risk_safety_eng) "An occurrence that actually or potentially jeopardizes the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of an information system or the information the system processes, stores, or transmits or that constitutes a violation or [...]" — Information-security sense. https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/incident

    serious incident

    probability

    severity

    failure

    failure mode

    • [38, Section 'Basic terms' (glossary list)] (risk_safety_eng) "The specific manner or way by which a failure occurs in terms of failure of the part, component, function, equipment, subsystem, or system under investigation. Depending on the type of FMEA performed, failure mode may be [...]" — Secondary source. Following sentences give piece-part, functional and process examples.

    failure effect

    • [38, Section 'Basic terms' (glossary list)] (risk_safety_eng) "Immediate consequences of a failure on operation, or more generally on the needs for the customer / user that should be fulfilled by the function but now is not, or not fully, fulfilled." — Secondary source.

    failure cause

    • [38, Section 'Basic terms' (glossary list), entry 'Failure cause and/or mechanism'] (risk_safety_eng) "Defects in requirements, design, process, quality control, handling or part application, which are the underlying cause or sequence of causes that initiate a process (mechanism) that leads to a failure mode over a certain time. A [...]" — Secondary source.

    error

    • [26, csrc term 'error'; NIST SP 800-160v1r1] (risk_safety_eng) "The difference between desired and actual performance or behavior of a system or system element." — https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/error

    unsafe control action

    • [7, Chapter 2, Identifying unsafe control actions] (risk_safety_eng) "An Unsafe Control Action (UCA) is a control action that, in a particular context and worst-case environment, will lead to a hazard." — Footnote 9 on the page. Handbook states a UCA has five parts: source, type (provided, not provided, too early or too late, stopped too soon or applied too long), control action, context, link to hazards.

    risk assessment
