    Disagreements and tensions across 51 AI-safety survey, taxonomy and agenda papers

    Source: papers/*.md, taxonomies/*.md. Themes: themes.md.

    Conventions - Cite as (Author Year), linked to the paper file. No suffix = file says [read-full-text] ([Manheim 2018][man18], [Hendrycks 2022][hen22], [Ngo 2022][ngo22], [Pan 2022][pan22], [Shah 2022][sha22], [Slattery 2024][sla24]). - (p) = claim rests only on a [read-partial] digest (mostly model-summariser output). (mk) = [not-fetched], model knowledge only ([Bostrom 2014][bos14], [Russell 2019][rus19]). - [outside] = fact not in the input files. - Tags: ERA = differs because of date or technology. REAL = persists among same-era papers. FRAMING = talking past each other. - Most papers state positions in digests, not full arguments. "Best case" lines are as the digests give them. - Many disagreements below are between papers that never address each other. I mark "implicit" where I infer the clash from separate claims.

    1. Catastrophic risk vs ongoing harms

    • Catastrophe first: [Bostrom 2014][bos14] (mk), [Critch 2020][cri20] (p), [Hendrycks 2022][hen22], [Bengio 2023][ben23] (p), [Bengio 2025 Scientist AI][bsc25] (p).
    • Ongoing / near-term first: [Weidinger 2021][wei21] (p) (21 LM risks; loss of control excluded), [Chan 2023][cha23] (p) (systemic, delayed harms), [Slattery 2024][sla24] (AI system safety and socioeconomic harms in over 75% of documents).
    • Both: [Hendrycks 2023][hen23] (p) (malicious use, race, organisational, rogue AI), [International AI Safety Report 2025][ben25] (p) and 2026 (p) (malicious use, malfunction, systemic), [Ngo 2022][ngo22] (x-risk plus deployed examples).
    • Best case, catastrophe side: one irreversible failure outweighs frequent small ones; act at small probability ([Everitt 2018][eve18] (p)).
    • Best case, ongoing side: harms are documented now; [Slattery 2024][sla24] finds post-deployment 62% vs pre-deployment 13% of coded risks.
    • Cross-fire (from reception sections):
      • Jarvis (2024) calls [Bengio 2023][ben23] warnings technological determinism and moral panic, but accepts present harms (p, snippet).
      • Raji and Dobbe say [Amodei 2016][amo16] vocabulary is useful but needs a socio-technical frame (p, listing only).
    • Bridge attempts: [Chan 2023][cha23] (p) links fairness/accountability and alignment communities; [Hendrycks 2023][hen23] (p) includes both.
    • Note: [Slattery 2024][sla24] counts 7% of documents for multi-agent risk and 3% for AI welfare, so even the "both" camp covers little of that ground.
    • Tag: REAL, with FRAMING. Same-era papers split (2021 Weidinger vs 2021 Hendrycks; 2023 Chan vs 2023 Carlsmith). Loss of control is often excluded by scope, not argued against.

    2. Agentic vs tool / non-agentic framing

    • Agency is the danger; avoid it: [Bengio 2025 Scientist AI][bsc25] (p) (non-agentic world model plus inference machine; safety improves with compute, per digest).
    • Agency is graded and already arriving: [Chan 2023][cha23] (p) (four traits: underspecification, directness of impact, goal-directedness, long-term planning).
    • Text-only is not safe by being text-only: [Kenton 2021][ken21] (p) (language agents can deceive and manipulate).
    • Intelligence is the wrong axis; prepotence is: [Critch 2020][cri20] (p).
    • No system type is clearly safe: [Bostrom 2014][bos14] (oracle, genie, sovereign, tool) (mk).
    • Goals arise inside models trained on non-agent objectives: [Ngo 2022][ngo22], [Hubinger 2019][hub19] (p), [Carlsmith 2023][car23] (p).
    • Reception clash: Douglas says the Scientist AI paper draws too sharp a line between agentic and non-agentic and overstates robustness; co-author macdermott replies that agency is set by training, not use; Viliam asks whether "model + way of use" can form an agent (all p, LW comments).
    • Best case, non-agentic: remove goals, remove the problem. Best case, against: any capable predictor can be wrapped in an agent loop (implicit; Viliam comment).
    • Tag: FRAMING (what counts as an agent) with REAL (whether a non-agentic design stays non-agentic in use).

    3. How likely is scheming, and how would we know

    • Speculative, unquantified: [Hubinger 2019][hub19] (p) ("still only speculative"; mesa-optimisers expected "mainly" in more advanced systems).
    • A number under conditions: [Carlsmith 2023][car23] (p): about 25% for a situationally aware, goal-directed, better-than-human model from pretraining plus RLHF-style training. Theory is weak evidence; run experiments.
    • Already seen in examples: [Park 2023][par23] (p) (CICERO, play-dead agents, sycophancy, unfaithful CoT). [Ngo 2022][ngo22] v8 adds o1 hacking and alignment faking (as the file reports).
    • Hypothetical for now: [International AI Safety Report 2025][ben25] (p) (broad consensus current systems lack capability; expert views on likelihood vary widely). Report 2026 (p): secondary sources say models can detect safety testing and conceal misalignment; not verified in the report text.
    • Design for it anyway: [Greenblatt 2023][gre23] (p), [Shah 2025][sha25] (p) ("possibly untrusted insider"), [Dalrymple 2024][dal24] (p) (tests cannot rule out deceptive alignment).
    • Counter: "Counting arguments provide no evidence for AI doom" attacks one of Carlsmith's argument families (p, title and snippet). Carlsmith replied that counting and simplicity arguments ignore the SGD path (p, search snippet).
    • Definition clash:
      • [Park 2023][par23] (p): deception needs no beliefs or goals, only systematic inducement of false beliefs.
      • [Carlsmith 2023][car23] (p) and [Hubinger 2019][hub19] (p): scheming needs situational awareness, beyond-episode goals, and an instrumental strategy.
      • So "AI already deceives" and "scheming is unlikely" can both be true.
    • Tied to methods: [Shah 2022][sha22] says many goal-misgeneralisation examples were built to show the effect, so they give little evidence about likelihood.
    • Tag: REAL (same-era 2023: Carlsmith vs Park vs IASR), ERA (evidence changes with newer models, per Ngo v8), FRAMING (definitions).

    4. Which technical approach

    Positions (all as the digests give them): - Reward modelling and RLHF as the core: [Leike 2018][lei18] (p) ("shovel-ready"), [Lambert 2025][lam25] (p) (textbook; no safety claim made in the digest). - RLHF is not enough: [Casper 2023][cas23] (p) ("basic, not hard, problems"; layered Swiss-cheese defence). - Scalable / amplified oversight: [Leech 2023][lee23] (p) (labs "betting the farm"), [Shah 2025][sha25] (p). - Control, assuming the model may be hostile: [Greenblatt 2023][gre23] (p), [Shah 2025][sha25] (p) (second line), [Leech 2024][lee24] (p) (field shifting from alignment to control). - Worst-case theory (ELK): [Christiano 2021][chr21] (p). Judge proposals by counterexamples. Contrast: empirical microcosm work over abstract solve-it-later work: [Hendrycks 2022][hen22]. Contrast: [Pan 2022][pan22] says trend extrapolation is not enough; expect phase transitions. - Formal guarantees: [Dalrymple 2024][dal24] (p). Critiques named in reception: Dickson ("Limitations on Formal Verification for AI Safety") and Hatfield-Dodds ("Formal Verification is Overrated"), via Ammann (p, titles only). - Assistance games / uncertain objectives: [Russell 2019][rus19] (mk). AF discussion "mixed" (p, search only). - Non-agentic design: [Bengio 2025 Scientist AI][bsc25] (p). - Monitoring CoT: [Korbak 2025][kor25] (p). Internal split in the comments: Hilton says earlier consensus favoured process-based over outcome-based RL; Shah says many OpenAI staff preferred outcome-based RL to avoid pressure on CoT (p). - Safety cases: [Clymer 2024][cly24] (p). Reception items are titles only: LW "Should the AI safety community prioritize safety cases?" and arXiv 2603.08760 (p, not read). - Authors who want breadth rather than a bet: [Ji 2023][ji23] (p) (forward plus backward cycle, RICE), [Anwar 2024][anw24] (p), [Leech 2023][lee23] (p) (de facto agenda "a hodgepodge" of partial methods; defence in depth). - Tension inside one proposal: [Everitt 2019][eve19] (p) expects isolating the reward process to fail at scale, so it wants design principles that remove the incentive. [Skalse 2022][ska22] (p) suggests learned rewards are aids, to be validated, not trusted. - Tag: REAL (same-era options coexist in 2023 and 2025), with ERA (control and CoT monitoring need LLM-era systems; RL-focused designs date from 2016-2021).

    5. Is interpretability tractable

    • Sceptical: [Rauker 2022][rau22] (p) (status quo "largely unproductive"; cherry-picking; does not scale). Co-author Casper's engineer's critique (p, via AF sequence). [Leech 2024][lee24] comments: Charbel, Bushnaq, Casper question whether mech interp yields actionable safety (p, comments not read).
    • Cautiously positive with caveats: [Bereska 2024][ber24] (p) (benefits in understanding, control, alignment; risks of capability gain and dual use). [Sharkey 2025][shk25] (p) (no natural units, SDL error, weak validation, must beat baselines).
    • Needed for assurance: [Anwar 2024][anw24] (p) (behavioural evals alone cannot assure; yet lists superposition and scaling as limits).
    • Supporting role only: [Shah 2025][sha25] (p), [Carlsmith 2023][car23] (p), [Leike 2018][lei18] (p), [Ji 2023][ji23] (p).
    • Best case for: mechanisms give causal evidence and let us read intent behind a clean surface. Best case against: tools rarely used in real engineering ([Rauker 2022][rau22] (p), claim dated 2023, "unverified for 2026").
    • Tag: REAL (2022 Rauker vs 2024 Bereska vs 2025 Sharkey all describe the same gaps), with a mild ERA trend: sparse-autoencoder methods appear after 2022 [outside].

    6. Governance vs technical work

    • Governance-led: [Bengio 2023][ben23] (p) (one third of R&D to safety; audits, liability), [Chan 2023][cha23] (p) (compute tracking, deployment thresholds), [Shevlane 2023][she23] (p) (evals as "critical governance infrastructure"), [International AI Safety Report 2025][ben25] (p) (no policy recommendations, marks gaps).
    • Technical-led with policy attached: [Hendrycks 2023][hen23] (p), [Dalrymple 2024][dal24] (p) (mandate guarantees above risk thresholds).
    • Technical only by scope: [Leech 2025][lee25] (p) (excludes policy, security, misuse), [Amodei 2016][amo16] (p).
    • Doubt about extrinsic rules: [Everitt 2018][eve18] (p) reports Baum arguing extrinsic regulation may backfire and intrinsic measures are better (p).
    • Link between the two: safety cases and evals ([Clymer 2024][cly24] (p), [Shevlane 2023][she23] (p), [Phuong 2024][phu24] (p)).
    • Tension: [IASR 2025][ben25] (p) says current risk-management tools have "highly significant limitations" and no quantitative guarantees; [Clymer 2024][cly24] (p) says trustworthiness arguments need fundamental research advances, and inability arguments fail at systems automating about half of cognitive tasks (digest, unverified).
    • Tag: FRAMING (separate audiences: researchers vs policy) with REAL on whether technical measures can carry a regulatory burden.

    7. Takeoff, continuity and warning time

    • Fast takeoff and decisive advantage: [Bostrom 2014][bos14] (mk). Expert AGI forecasts "vary widely": [Everitt 2018][eve18] (p) (medians 2040-2061).
    • Approximate continuity as a working assumption: [Shah 2025][sha25] (p) ("capability grows smoothly enough to give warning").
    • Rapid growth and little time: [Bengio 2023][ben23] (p) (generalist AI possibly within the decade). [IASR 2025][ben25] (p) reports experts disagree on the pace (slow, rapid, extremely rapid).
    • Phase transitions in behaviour under scaling: [Pan 2022][pan22].
    • Tag: ERA ([Bostrom 2014][bos14] reasons from self-improvement; 2025 GDM from scaling [outside inference]), with REAL within 2025.

    8. Does more capability make safety easier or harder

    • Harder: [Pan 2022][pan22] (more capable agents hack more), [Weng 2024][wen24] (p) (hacking skills generalise), [Critch 2020][cri20] (p) (races), [Hendrycks 2023][hen23] (p) (competitive pressure).
    • Mixed: [Gao 2022][gao22] (p) (larger policies gain less from optimisation but overoptimise similarly; KL penalty did not improve the frontier, per digest).
    • Easier: [Bengio 2025 Scientist AI][bsc25] (p) (safety improves with compute, per digest; verify in text). [Hendrycks 2022][hen22]: safety metrics often rise with capability, so researchers must measure the capabilities externality.
    • Autonomy: [Greenblatt 2023][gre23] (p) says control weakens as the capability gap grows; [Clymer 2024][cly24] (p) expects reliance to move from inability to control to trustworthiness to deference.
    • Tag: REAL.

    9. What counts as reward hacking, misspecification, misgeneralisation

    • Spec gaming covers design, domain assumptions, tampering: [Krakovna 2020][kra20] (p) (five causes in the page summary). Tampering is narrower than misspecification: [Everitt 2019][eve19] (p).
    • Formal view: hackability is a property of a proxy-true pair over a policy set; non-trivial unhackable pairs exist for finite policy sets but are equivalent over sets with an open subset: [Skalse 2022][ska22] (p). Authors say the definition is arguably too strict.
    • Goal misgeneralisation: spec correct, learned goal wrong ([Shah 2022][sha22], [Langosco 2021][lan21] (p)); the term "misgeneralisation" is called a misnomer for hiding two failure modes (So8res 2023, p, snippet). Authors flag InstructGPT as uncertain ([Shah 2022][sha22]).
    • Inner vs outer alignment: [Hubinger 2019][hub19] (p), versus an objective-behaviour view where Everitt argues behavioural objective may predict better off-distribution (p, AF comments). Shah doubts mechanistic-search assumptions; Mikulik defends the internal-structure view.
    • Goodhart: four modes ([Manheim 2018][man18]) vs "errors in specifying the objective" (Russell suggestion, in [Krakovna 2018][kra18] (p) comments).
    • Breadth: [Weng 2024][wen24] (p) includes in-context hacking and LLM-judge bias, wider than the RL definitions.
    • Tag: FRAMING. Same phenomena get different names and boundaries.

    10. How the surveys cut the field

    Cuts, from the 17 taxonomy files and papers: | Cut by | Examples | |---|---| | Risk source (who or what causes harm) | [Hendrycks 2023][hen23] (p) (4 sources); [GDM 2025][sha25] (p) (misuse, misalignment, mistakes, structural); [IASR 2026][ben26] (p) (misuse, malfunction, systemic); [Slattery 2024][sla24] (entity, intent, timing) | | Harm type | [Weidinger 2021][wei21] (p) (6 areas); [Slattery 2024][sla24] (7 domains, 24 subdomains in latest version) | | Failure mechanism | [Manheim 2018][man18] (4 Goodhart modes); [Carlsmith 2023][car23] (p) (training gamers vs saints, 3 prerequisites); Omohundro 2008 taxonomy (basic drives) | | Where in the learning loop | [Amodei 2016][amo16] (p) (objective, oversight cost, learning behaviour); DeepMind 2018 taxonomy (specification, robustness, assurance) | | Research approach | [Hendrycks 2021][hen21] (p) (robustness, monitoring, alignment, systemic); Singapore Consensus 2025 taxonomy (assess, develop, control); Shallow Review 2025 (p) (black-box vs white-box); Anthropic 2025 taxonomy (evaluate, control, oversee, harden) | | Kind of difficulty | [Anwar 2024][anw24] (p) (science, methods, sociotechnical) | | Life-cycle | [Ji 2023][ji23] (p) (forward: training; backward: assurance, governance) | | Discipline | UK AISI Alignment Project 2025 taxonomy (fields to recruit) |

    Consequences for comparing papers: - Same item, different home. Interpretability is a "white-box" area ([Leech 2025][lee25] (p)), a backward-alignment assurance tool ([Ji 2023][ji23] (p)), a "supporting" line ([Shah 2025][sha25] (p)) and a challenge ([Anwar 2024][anw24] (p)). - Same name, different scope. "Alignment" is intent alignment in [Anwar 2024][anw24] (p), a four-objective RICE set in [Ji 2023][ji23] (p), and the goal-learning problem in [Leike 2018][lei18] (p). - Control-vs-understanding cut ([Leech 2023][lee23] (p)) became control-vs-alignment emphasis ([Leech 2024][lee24] (p)) and then black-box-vs-white-box ([Leech 2025][lee25] (p)). The three taxonomies differ, so year-on-year comparison is unsafe. - Singapore 2025 puts societal resilience inside Control (3.3). The 2026 edition structure, as the S note describes it, lists it as a pillar; 2026 edition partly unverified. - Scope exclusions drive coverage: reviews exclude misuse and policy ([Leech 2025][lee25] (p)); [Weidinger 2021][wei21] (p) excludes loss of control; [Slattery 2024][sla24] counts both. - Risk-vs-approach asymmetry: risk-source cuts say what to fear, approach cuts say what to do. Few papers map approach to risk ([Hendrycks 2023][hen23] (p) adds suggestions per source; [Carlsmith 2023][car23] (p) lists experiments per prerequisite). - Tag: FRAMING.

    11. Critiques found in reception sections

    Only items the files report. Searches were thin; absence is not evidence.

    12. Corrections to the user's earlier notes ("S note")

    Every place a paper or taxonomy file says the source contradicts, or fails to confirm, the S note. "Fix" = file says to change the S note.

    12.1 Contradicted

    • Anthropic recommended directions 2025: S note says 15 sub-directions; the page shows 14 (taxonomy of Anthropic 2025).
    • [Carlsmith 2023][car23] (p): S note names the third prerequisite "Goal-guarding strategy" with a quote. The fetch shows "Power-Motivated Instrumental Strategy" with a different quote. The note's name and quote are unconfirmed. Used the fetched name. Terminal/instrumental split labels also differ slightly in wording (taxonomy of [Carlsmith 2023][car23]). Length: 127 pages in S note; fetch says about 80,000 words (unverified).
    • [Skalse 2022][ska22] (p): S note says unhackability is achievable only when one reward is constant or the two are nearly identical for "most reward pairs". The paper is narrower: over all stochastic policies (or any set with an open subset) non-trivial unhackable pairs are equivalent; over finite sets (including deterministic policies) non-trivial unhackable pairs always exist.
    • [Lambert 2025][lam25] (p): S note chapter list differs. The real book has no chapter titled "Constitutional AI" or "Open questions"; "Policy gradients" is chapter 6, "Reinforcement Learning". Chapters 6, 9, 11, 12, 13, 17 subsections not read. The list is the current repo, not necessarily v1.
    • [Leike 2018][lei18] (p): S note lists "agent narration" and "off-policy feedback" as headings; the text has "Natural language" (5.5). Section 5.2 not captured, so "off-policy" is unverified as a heading.
    • [Slattery 2024][sla24]: S note numbers (43 taxonomies, 700+ risks, 23 subdomains) are version 1. Latest: 74 documents, 1,725 risks, 24 subdomains (domain taxonomy: 7 domains).
    • [Everitt 2019][eve19] (p): S note lists "model-based rewards"; the paper names them "belief-based rewards" (4.3). Fix the S note. Also: S note types it "agenda"; it is a formal-analysis paper with design principles.
    • [Chan 2023][cha23] (p): S note lists harms as systemic, collusion, misuse, erosion of control; the fetched headings differ. Fetched headings are the verified ones. Authors: S note says 22 total.
    • [Korbak 2025][kor25] (p): S note lists "process supervision" as a degrader of monitorability; the paper's heading is "Direct supervision of CoT".
    • [GDM 2025][sha25] (p): S note calls it "Red teaming and misuse stress tests"; fetched: "Misuse stress tests". S note "Uncertainty estimation"; fetched "Uncertainty". The AF list and arXiv HTML headings are two views, not reconciled.
    • [Ji 2023][ji23] (p): S note examples "risk extrapolation, mode connectivity" not seen; fetched has Cross-Distribution Aggregation and Mode Connectivity.
    • Omohundro 2008 taxonomy: S note lists 4 drives; summariser output shows 5 (adds "Goal clarification"), seen only in summariser output; not checked against raw text.
    • Singapore Consensus 2025 taxonomy: S note lists societal resilience as a fourth pillar (2026 structure); the 2025 PDF puts it at 3.3 inside Control. S note's "Verification and validation" (2.3) corrected to "Verification".
    • Shallow Review 2025 (p): S note and index say 86 agendas (827 items classified). The YAML gives 81 leaves, 74 excluding Labs. Not resolved.
    • [Ngo 2022][ngo22]: S note date 2022-08; text read is v8 (4 May 2025); v1 is 2022-08. The 2025 version contains new evidence not in v1. (arXiv ID 2209.00626 suggests September 2022 [outside].)
    • [Krakovna 2020][kra20] (p): S note lists three causes; the page summary lists five (reward shaping that changes the optimal policy, incomplete outcome specification, inaccurate learned reward models, simulator bugs, tampering). Also S note types it "taxonomy"; it is a short explainer blog.
    • [Everitt 2018][eve18] (p): the S note's "taxonomy" is only the section list.
