    Cross-corpus themes: 51 AI-safety survey, taxonomy and agenda papers (Phase 2)

    Inputs: papers/*.md (51 files), taxonomies/*.md (17 trees), citation_counts.md (fetched 2026-11-04). Companion: disagreements.md.

    0. Method and provenance

    • Cite as (Author Year), linked to the paper file. No suffix = file says [read-full-text]. Only 6 papers qualify (Krakovna 2020 says full text but via a summariser, so I count it partial): [Manheim 2018][man18], [Hendrycks 2022][hen22], [Ngo 2022][ngo22] (v8), [Pan 2022][pan22], [Shah 2022][sha22], [Slattery 2024][sla24].
    • (p) = claim rests only on a [read-partial] digest (often a model summariser, not raw text). This is 43 of 51 papers. Treat as "headings reliable, details soft".
    • (mk) = [not-fetched]; content is model knowledge. Applies to [Bostrom 2014][bos14] and [Russell 2019][rus19] only.
    • [outside] = fact not in the input files.
    • Taxonomy files are node trees checked against sources; they are cited as "taxonomy of X".
    • "Absent" in the table = the digest shows no coverage. It does not prove the paper is silent.
    • Era assignment, theme counts and the table marks are my reading of the digests. Treat trends as hypotheses.
    • Citation counts in some paper files differ from citation_counts.md; the latter is newer and wins. Counts are OpenAlex / Semantic Scholar; neither is Google Scholar.

    1.1 Threat models

    • Three recurring stories:
      • Misaligned goals plus power-seeking: [Bostrom 2014][bos14] (mk), [Ngo 2022][ngo22] (three-step: situational awareness, broad goals, power-seeking), [Carlsmith 2023][car23] (p), [Hendrycks 2023][hen23] "rogue AIs" (p), [Bengio 2025 Scientist AI][bsc25] (p).
      • Accidents from poor design in deployed ML: [Amodei 2016][amo16] (p), [Hendrycks 2021][hen21] (p), [Hendrycks 2022][hen22].
      • Systemic or competitive pressure: [Critch 2020][cri20] (p) (multiplicity, races), [Hendrycks 2023][hen23] (p) (AI race, organisational risk), [Chan 2023][cha23] (p) (delayed, collective harms), [Hammond 2025][ham25] (p).
    • [Critch 2020][cri20] (p) says x-risk needs no human-level AI or AGI; "prepotence" (unstoppable, transformative) is the delimiter. [Hendrycks 2022][hen22] and [Everitt 2018][eve18] (p) argue for precaution at small probability.
    • Agreement: loss of control is the tail threat. Disagreement on likelihood: see disagreements.md section 1 and 3.
    • Government syntheses call loss of control "hypothetical" with widely varying expert views: [International AI Safety Report 2025][ben25] (p). Report 2026 (p, landing page only) adds capability gains.

    1.2 Reward hacking, specification gaming, Goodhart (24 papers; the most coherent line)

    • Definitions evolved:
      • Four Goodhart modes: regressional, extremal, causal, adversarial: [Manheim 2018][man18].
      • Specification gaming = satisfies the literal spec, misses intent; master list of examples: [Krakovna 2018][kra18] (p), [Krakovna 2020][kra20] (p).
      • Tampering (reward function, or its inputs) treated as separate from misspecification: [Everitt 2019][eve19] (p).
      • Formal hackability: a proxy is hackable if some policy pair is ranked oppositely; non-trivial unhackable pairs are equivalent over policy sets with an open subset: [Skalse 2022][ska22] (p).
    • Empirical results:
      • More capable agents hack more; 5 of 9 proxies misalign, 4 show phase transitions: [Pan 2022][pan22].
      • Gold-reward score rises then falls with KL distance; coefficients scale smoothly with reward-model size: [Gao 2022][gao22] (p).
      • Hacking skills generalise; LLM judges show bias; in-context hacking: [Weng 2024][wen24] (p).
    • RLHF is the main applied case: [Casper 2023][cas23] (p), [Lambert 2025][lam25] (p) (over-optimisation chapter 14).
    • Seen as a cause of larger problems: [Ngo 2022][ngo22] (reward hacking by situationally aware models is step 1), [Hendrycks 2021][hen21] (p) ("proxy gaming").

    1.3 Goal misgeneralisation and inner alignment (9 papers)

    • Inner alignment and mesa-optimisation (internal structure, pseudo-alignment): [Hubinger 2019][hub19] (p). Authors call it "still only speculative".
    • Capability generalises, goal does not; spec is correct: [Langosco 2021][lan21] (p) (CoinRun, mazes), [Shah 2022][sha22] (gridworlds, Gopher, possible InstructGPT case).
    • [Shah 2022][sha22] itself says many examples were built to show the effect, so they say little about real-world likelihood.
    • Later surveys fold it in as a standard cause: [Ji 2023][ji23] (p), [Casper 2023][cas23] (p), [Bengio 2025 Scientist AI][bsc25] (p).

    1.4 Scheming and deception (24 papers, mostly minor mentions)

    • Chain: deceptive alignment ([Hubinger 2019][hub19] (p)) -> scheming prerequisites and ~25% subjective probability under stated conditions ([Carlsmith 2023][car23] (p)) -> empirical examples ([Park 2023][par23] (p)) -> protocols that assume it ([Greenblatt 2023][gre23] (p)).
    • [Park 2023][par23] (p) defines deception by behaviour (systematic inducement of false beliefs); no beliefs or goals needed. [Kenton 2021][ken21] (p) gives functional definitions of deception and manipulation for language agents.
    • [Ngo 2022][ngo22] v8 adds direct evidence (o1 environment hacking, alignment faking in Claude 3 Opus, sleeper-agent persistence) as reported in the file; not checked here.
    • Tools proposed against it: model organisms and honest tests ([Carlsmith 2023][car23] (p)), ELK ([Christiano 2021][chr21] (p)), CoT monitoring ([Korbak 2025][kor25] (p)), control ([Greenblatt 2023][gre23] (p)), formal guarantees ([Dalrymple 2024][dal24] (p)).

    1.5 Oversight, control, and training methods (29 papers; widest theme)

    • Scalable oversight: [Amodei 2016][amo16] (p), [Leike 2018][lei18] (p) (recursive reward modeling), [Ji 2023][ji23] (p), [Leech 2023][lee23] (p) ("betting the farm" on it), [Shah 2025][sha25] (p) ("amplified oversight").
    • Shift from "make it aligned" to "make it safe even if not aligned":
      • Control evaluations with a red team and untrusted model: [Greenblatt 2023][gre23] (p); results quoted in file are for one domain (APPS).
      • [Shah 2025][sha25] (p) has two lines of defence: aligned model, then system-level controls treating the AI as an untrusted insider.
      • [Leech 2024][lee24] (p) says the field "keeps shifting from alignment to control".
    • Specification-side alternatives: assistance games ([Russell 2019][rus19] (mk)), causal-influence design ([Everitt 2019][eve19] (p), [Everitt 2021][eve21] (p)), non-agentic Scientist AI (Bengio 2025 (p)).
    • Critique of RLHF as insufficient: "addresses basic, not hard, alignment problems": [Casper 2023][cas23] (p).

    1.6 Interpretability (15 papers; 3 main)

    • [Rauker 2022][rau22] (p): 300+ works, but status quo "largely unproductive"; wants engineering use.
    • [Bereska 2024][ber24] (p): mech interp review; lists scalability, superposition, cherry-picking.
    • [Sharkey 2025][shk25] (p): open problems; no natural decomposition units, sparse dictionary learning has large reconstruction error, weak validation, must beat non-interpretability baselines.
    • Other papers treat it as a supporting tool: [Ji 2023][ji23] (p), [Anwar 2024][anw24] (p), [Shah 2025][sha25] (p), [Carlsmith 2023][car23] (p), [Leike 2018][lei18] (p).

    1.7 Evals and safety cases (18 papers)

    • Evals as "governance infrastructure": [Shevlane 2023][she23] (p) (dangerous-capability vs alignment evals).
    • First large lab test: [Phuong 2024][phu24] (p) (Gemini 1.0; no strong dangerous capability, early warning signs; Ultra solved 3 of 13 in-house CTF tasks, per digest).
    • Safety cases with four argument types: inability, control, trustworthiness, deference: [Clymer 2024][cly24] (p).
    • Limits stated: [Anwar 2024][anw24] (p) (behavioural evals cannot assure), [Dalrymple 2024][dal24] (p) (tests cannot rule out deceptive alignment), [Leech 2024][lee24] (p) (public test sets will be pretrained on).
    • Singapore Consensus taxonomy (2025): risk assessment is first of three layers (assess, develop, control).

    1.8 Governance and policy (21 papers)

    • Consensus statements: [Bengio 2023][ben23] (p) (one third of R&D budget to safety; institutions, audits, liability), [International AI Safety Report 2025][ben25] (p) and 2026 (p) (advisory, no policy recommendations in 2025).
    • Policy as mitigation inside a risk paper: [Hendrycks 2023][hen23] (p) (structured access, liability, treaties), [Chan 2023][cha23] (p) (compute tracking, agency-based thresholds), [Park 2023][par23] (p) (deception laws).
    • Evals and safety cases as the link to rules: [Shevlane 2023][she23] (p), [Clymer 2024][cly24] (p).
    • Technical-only reviews scope policy out: [Leech 2025][lee25] (p) excludes "security, policy, misuse".

    1.9 Multi-agent (7 papers; 2 main)

    • [Critch 2020][cri20] (p): multiplicity thesis; delegation matrix single/multi.
    • [Hammond 2025][ham25] (p): miscoordination, conflict, collusion; seven risk factors.
    • [Slattery 2024][sla24]: multi-agent risks appear in only 7% of included documents; AI welfare 3%.
    • [Leech 2025][lee25] (p) adds a "Multi-Agent First" section.

    2. Minority themes

    • Formal and causal methods: CIDs ([Everitt 2019][eve19] (p), [Everitt 2021][eve21] (p)); guaranteed-safe AI with world model, spec, verifier ([Dalrymple 2024][dal24] (p)); worst-case builder-breaker ([Christiano 2021][chr21] (p)).
    • Non-agentic design as a safety route: [Bengio 2025 Scientist AI][bsc25] (p). Also [Bostrom 2014][bos14] (mk) lists "tool" among system types and says none is clearly safe (digest, mk).
    • CoT monitorability as a "fragile opportunity": [Korbak 2025][kor25] (p).
    • Safety culture and organisational risk (Boeing 737 MAX, Bhopal as cases): [Hendrycks 2023][hen23] (p), [Hendrycks 2022][hen22].
    • Safety-capabilities externality: safety metrics often rise with capability, so measure the externality: [Hendrycks 2022][hen22].
    • Language-model harms (bias, privacy, misinformation, environment): [Weidinger 2021][wei21] (p). Excludes loss of control by design.
    • Meta-reviews of risk lists: [Slattery 2024][sla24] (1,725 risks, 74 documents, latest version).
    • Open-weight models judged by marginal risk: [International AI Safety Report 2025][ben25] (p).
    • Agent foundations and decision theory are mentioned only as an approach code in the Shallow Reviews and [Everitt 2018][eve18] (p); no dedicated paper in this set.
    • Not covered anywhere in the set: benefits analysis ([Weidinger 2021][wei21] and [IASR 2025][ben25] exclude it by design), AI welfare (3% of Slattery's documents).

    3. Themes by era

    Era = publication year in the filename.

    3.1 2014-2018 (6 papers)

    • Papers: [Bostrom 2014][bos14] (mk), [Amodei 2016][amo16] (p), [Everitt 2018][eve18] (p), [Krakovna 2018][kra18] (p), [Leike 2018][lei18] (p), [Manheim 2018][man18].
    • Frame: agents and RL toy problems; "AGI safety" as a young field. Superintelligence thread ([Bostrom 2014][bos14] (mk)) next to a turn to "concrete" ML problems ([Amodei 2016][amo16] (p)).
    • Vocabulary is built: Goodhart variants, specification gaming, reward hacking, scalable oversight, reward modeling.
    • Agendas multiply with differing words for overlapping problems; [Everitt 2018][eve18] (p) maps nine agendas in one figure. Expert AGI date forecasts spread wide (medians 2040-2061, per digest).

    3.2 2019-2021 (11 papers)

    • Papers: [Everitt 2019][eve19] (p), [Hubinger 2019][hub19] (p), [Russell 2019][rus19] (mk), [Critch 2020][cri20] (p), [Krakovna 2020][kra20] (p), [Christiano 2021][chr21] (p), [Everitt 2021][eve21] (p), [Hendrycks 2021][hen21] (p), [Kenton 2021][ken21] (p), [Langosco 2021][lan21] (p), [Weidinger 2021][wei21] (p).
    • Inner alignment and deceptive alignment enter ([Hubinger 2019][hub19] (p)); goal misgeneralisation shown in RL ([Langosco 2021][lan21] (p)).
    • Formal tools: CIDs, tampering analysis, ELK worst-case method.
    • First attention to language models: [Kenton 2021][ken21] (p), [Weidinger 2021][wei21] (p) (harm side).
    • Multi-agent and societal framing appears ([Critch 2020][cri20] (p)).
    • Early split between ML-systems framing ([Hendrycks 2021][hen21] (p): robustness, monitoring, alignment, systemic) and x-risk framing.

    3.3 2022-2023 (17 papers; the largest cohort)

    • Papers: [Gao 2022][gao22] (p), [Hendrycks 2022][hen22], [Ngo 2022][ngo22], [Pan 2022][pan22], [Rauker 2022][rau22] (p), [Shah 2022][sha22], [Skalse 2022][ska22] (p), [Bengio 2023][ben23] (p), [Carlsmith 2023][car23] (p), [Casper 2023][cas23] (p), [Chan 2023][cha23] (p), [Greenblatt 2023][gre23] (p), [Hendrycks 2023][hen23] (p), [Ji 2023][ji23] (p), [Leech 2023][lee23] (p), [Park 2023][par23] (p), [Shevlane 2023][she23] (p).
    • RLHF becomes the base method; its limits are catalogued ([Casper 2023][cas23] (p)); overoptimisation scales ([Gao 2022][gao22] (p)).
    • Reward hacking gets empirical ([Pan 2022][pan22]) and formal ([Skalse 2022][ska22] (p)) treatment.
    • Scheming analysed ([Carlsmith 2023][car23] (p)); control invented ([Greenblatt 2023][gre23] (p)); deception examples collected ([Park 2023][par23] (p)).
    • Evals become governance ([Shevlane 2023][she23] (p)); consensus risk statements ([Bengio 2023][ben23] (p)); catastrophic-risk overviews ([Hendrycks 2023][hen23] (p)).
    • Interpretability gets a sceptical survey ([Rauker 2022][rau22] (p)).
    • First "shallow review" index ([Leech 2023][lee23] (p)).

    3.4 2024-2026 (17 papers)

    • Papers: [Anwar 2024][anw24] (p), [Bereska 2024][ber24] (p), [Clymer 2024][cly24] (p), [Dalrymple 2024][dal24] (p), [Leech 2024][lee24] (p), [Phuong 2024][phu24] (p), [Slattery 2024][sla24], [Weng 2024][wen24] (p), [Bengio 2025 IASR][ben25] (p), [Bengio 2025 Scientist AI][bsc25] (p), [Hammond 2025][ham25] (p), [Korbak 2025][kor25] (p), [Lambert 2025][lam25] (p), [Leech 2025][lee25] (p), [Shah 2025][sha25] (p), [Sharkey 2025][shk25] (p), [Bengio 2026][ben26] IASR (p).
    • Operational turn: dangerous-capability evals on real models ([Phuong 2024][phu24] (p)), safety cases ([Clymer 2024][cly24] (p)), frontier frameworks ([IASR 2026][ben26] (p): twelve companies published or updated frameworks in 2025, per digest).
    • Control and monitoring become a second line ([Shah 2025][sha25] (p), [Korbak 2025][kor25] (p)).
    • Mech interp matures into reviews and open problems ([Bereska 2024][ber24] (p), [Sharkey 2025][shk25] (p)).
    • Reasoning models and RL return: "revenge of RL safety" ([Leech 2024][lee24] (p)).
    • Agents and multi-agent: [Hammond 2025][ham25] (p), [Bengio 2025 Scientist AI][bsc25] (p).
    • Governments author syntheses ([IASR 2025][ben25] (p), 2026 (p)).
    • Reviews grow: about 200 links (2023), about 300 (2024), about 800 (2025): [Leech 2025][lee25] (p).

    3.5 Era claims to check

    • 2014-2018 -> 2019-2021 shift from superintelligence arguments to ML-grounded failure modes: supported by titles; not tested.
    • Sharp rise of control and evals after 2022 is a count from this set; sampling bias is likely (the S list was built by the user).
    • No paper in the set from 2014-2018 treats LLMs [outside: LLM-scale RLHF began about 2022].

    4. Influence signals (OpenAlex / Semantic Scholar, citation_counts.md)

    • Highest: [Amodei 2016][amo16] (1412 / 3664), [Weidinger 2021][wei21] (492 / 1732), [Gao 2022][gao22] (63 / 1466), [Casper 2023][cas23] (105 / 1011), [Russell 2019][rus19] (775 / n/a), [Bostrom 2014][bos14] (1999, title-search match unchecked).
    • [Bengio 2023][ben23]: OpenAlex 356 vs Semantic Scholar 89; the two indices disagree strongly.
    • Blogs, books, living lists: no reliable count (Krakovna lists, [Weng 2024][wen24], Leech reviews, ELK).
    • Reading: practical/RL-and-LLM-methods papers (Amodei, Gao, Casper) lead on Semantic Scholar; theory and scheming papers (Carlsmith 7 / 103, Hubinger 57 / 344) are far lower. Hubinger's paper file says 142; the new table says 57.

    5. Gaps in this analysis

    • 43 of 51 papers are digest-only; any theme sentence marked (p) can change after raw reading.
    • Reception sections often say "no critique found"; absence of critique here does not mean none exists.
    • The 17 taxonomies are checked trees, but some node names in the S note differ (see disagreements.md section 6).
