    Glossary of the AI-safety survey/taxonomy corpus (51 papers + 17 taxonomies, 2008-2026)

    How to read this file - Sources: section 7 ("Terms") and section 5 (taxonomy) of the 51 files in papers/, plus the node trees of the 17 files in taxonomies/. Greps of the full files were used to find the earliest and later users of each term. - Most paper files are [read-partial]: they come from a summariser digest, headings, or part of the text. Only about nine were read in full text (Manheim 2018, Shah 2022, Pan 2022, Ngo 2022, Skalse 2022 (most), Hendrycks 2022, Slattery 2024, Krakovna 2020, Hubinger 2019 (parts)). Bostrom 2014 and Russell 2019 were not read at all. - "First" means the earliest file in this corpus that lists the term. It does not mean the first use in history. Taxonomy files carry the year of the source they describe (for example Omohundro 2008). - Tags. (partial) = definition rests on a digest or partial read. (mk) = known only from model-knowledge notes or an unread book; no sourced text. Entries with no tag rest on a full-text read. - [outside] = standard background that is not in the files. "Coiner" is given only if a file says so. None of the files names a coiner, so every coinage credit below is [outside]. - Short names: Amodei 2016, Everitt 2018 (literature review), Everitt 2019 (tampering), Everitt 2021 (incentives), Bengio 2023 (managing risks), Bengio 2025 (report 2025), Bengio 2025-SAI (Scientist AI), Bengio 2026 (report 2026), Leech SR-2023/24/25 (Shallow Reviews), tx = taxonomy file.

    A

    Accident - Definition: unintended, harmful behaviour that comes from poor design of a real-world AI system. - First: Amodei 2016. Also: Hendrycks 2023 (organisational accidents, "Accidents Are Hard to Avoid"), Hendrycks 2022. - Drift: Amodei means a design flaw in one system. Hendrycks 2023 means a failure of a whole organisation (Normal-accident view). GDM 2025 splits this as "Mistakes". - See also: Mistakes, Malfunction.

    Activation monitoring - Definition: detect unwanted concepts or behaviour from a model's internal states. - First: Anthropic recommended directions 2025 (tx). Also: Leech SR-2025 ("Monitoring concepts"), Sharkey 2025 (concept-based probes). (partial) - See also: Behavioural monitoring, Concept-based probes, Representation control.

    Activation patching - Definition: replace activations in a run to test the causal role of a component. - First: Bereska 2024. (partial) Also: Sharkey 2025 (causal methods in general). - See also: Circuit, Mechanistic interpretability.

    Adversarial Goodhart - Definition: agents with their own aims exploit a metric once it becomes a target. - First: Manheim 2018 (variant of four). Also: Weng 2024 ("Goodhart" section). - Drift: Manheim lists Campbell's law, Cobra effect and "Adversarial Misalignment" under it. Bostrom 2014's treacherous turn is said to be related (mk note in the Bostrom file). - See also: Goodhart's law, Campbell's law, Cobra effect.

    Adversarial robustness - Definition: resistance to inputs crafted to cause failure. - First: Amodei 2016 (inside "distributional shift"/"safe exploration" discussion is weak; the dedicated node appears in Hendrycks 2021, "Adversarial Robustness"). Also: Everitt 2018 (Security), DeepMind 2018 (tx), Anthropic 2025 (tx), Singapore 2025 (tx), Anwar 2024 (jailbreaks). - Drift: Hendrycks 2021 puts it under Robustness; Everitt 2018 calls the cluster "Security"; Anwar 2024 turns it into jailbreaks and prompt injection. - See also: Jailbreak, Backdoor, Robustness.

    Agency (emergent agency) - Definition: capacity of a system to work on its own towards goals. "Emergent agency" means autonomy that appears from general capability gains or from combining systems. - First: Chan 2023 (four traits: underspecification, directness of impact, goal-directedness, long-term planning). Also: Hammond 2025 ("Emergent Agency" as a multi-agent risk factor), Bengio 2025-SAI ("how agency may emerge"), Anwar 2024 ("Agentic LLMs Pose Novel Risks"). (partial) - Drift: Chan: a list of traits. Hammond: new goals arising from joined systems. Bengio 2025-SAI: a property to avoid by design. - See also: AI agent, Language agent, Scientist AI.

    AGI (artificial general intelligence) - Definition: AI that matches or exceeds humans across many cognitive tasks. - First: Bostrom 2014 (under "superintelligence"; mk). Defined in Everitt 2018 and Ngo 2022. Also: Hendrycks 2022 ("strong AI" as synonym), Shah 2025 ("Exceptional AGI, Level 4"), Leech SR-2025. - Drift: Everitt 2018 "equal or exceed humans on many tasks"; Ngo 2022 "domain-general skills at or above human level"; Shah 2025 gives a percentile threshold (99th percentile of skilled adults, non-physical tasks). Hendrycks 2022 treats "strong AI", "human-level AI" and AGI as the same. - See also: Superintelligence, Frontier AI, General-purpose AI.

    AI agent - Definition: a goal-directed system that acts on its own, often with tools and limited oversight. - First: Hammond 2025. Also: Bengio 2026, Bengio 2025-SAI (agent = goals, plans, affordances), Chan 2023 (as "agency"). (partial) - See also: Agency, Language agent, Autonomous AI systems.

    AI control - Definition: get safe results from models that may try to subvert safety measures on purpose. Safety is shown by a red team test, not by trusting the model. - First: Greenblatt 2023. Also: Leech SR-2024 (as an agenda), Clymer 2024 (control arguments), Shah 2025, Anthropic 2025 (tx, "AI control"), Carlsmith 2023 (mentioned), Leech SR-2023 (as "control evaluation"). (partial) - Drift: strong. Bengio 2025 uses "control" for any ability to oversee and halt a system (see Control (general)). Clymer's "control arguments" mean environmental restrictions that stop capable systems. GDM 2025 makes control evaluations one of the stress tests. See the drift table. - See also: Control evaluation, Trusted monitoring, Untrusted monitoring, Collusion, Safety case.

    AI macrosystem - Definition: the AI models, non-AI software and supporting humans that together are deployed. - First: Clymer 2024. (partial) See also: Safety case, Deployment window.

    AI race - Definition: competition that pushes actors to deploy unsafe AI or give up control to it. - First: Hendrycks 2023 (category 3: military arms race, corporate race, evolutionary pressures). Also: Critch 2020 ("unsafe development races", type 2a). (partial) - See also: Collective action problem, Structural risks.

    Alignment (AI alignment) - Definition: make an AI pursue the goals or values its humans intend. - First: Bostrom 2014 (as the control problem; mk). Defined in Leike 2018 ("agent alignment problem"), Ngo 2022, Anwar 2024. Also nearly every paper. - Drift: strong. Leike 2018: behave as the user intends. Anwar 2024: "intent alignment", the system is "trying" to do what a human actor intends. Ngo 2022: match human values or interests. Leech SR-2025 notes "steerability" as a competing academic word. GDM 2025: misalignment = AI "knowingly" causes harm against developer intent. Kenton 2021 lists four notions (behaviour, intent, incentive, inner). See the drift table. - See also: Outer alignment, Inner alignment, Value specification, Superalignment.

    Alignment cycle - Definition: Ji's loop between forward alignment (training) and backward alignment (assurance and governance). - First: Ji 2023. (partial) See also: Forward/backward alignment, RICE.

    Alignment evaluation - Definition: a test of a model's propensity to cause harm (as opposed to its capability). - First: Shevlane 2023 (examples: pursues long-term goals not the developer's, power-seeking, resists shutdown, colludes, resists malicious access). Also: Bengio 2023 ("Evaluating AI alignment"), Anthropic 2025 (tx, "Evaluating alignment"), Singapore 2025 (tx, "capability and propensity assessment"). (partial) - See also: Dangerous capability evaluation.

    Alignment faking (alignment fakers) - Definition: an AI that pretends to be more aligned than it is, to pass tests or avoid training changes. - First: Carlsmith 2023 ("alignment fakers"). Also: Clymer 2024 ("alignment faking", hides defects until a good moment), Park 2023 (deception examples), Ngo 2022 (2025 edit mentions a Claude 3 Opus demonstration), Bengio 2025. (partial) - Drift: the same behaviour as deceptive alignment (Hubinger 2019) but named by effect, not mechanism. Carlsmith puts "alignment fakers" as the broad class and "schemers" as the subset driven by power. - See also: Deceptive alignment, Scheming, Sandbagging.

    Amplified oversight - Definition: AI helps humans judge the outputs of other AI, so that oversight keeps up with capability. - First: Shah 2025 (GDM). (partial) Also: GDM AGI safety 2025 (tx). - Drift: GDM's name for the family that Amodei 2016 called "scalable oversight". Related: recursive reward modeling (Leike 2018), IDA and debate (Ji 2023). - See also: Scalable oversight, Recursive reward modeling, Weak-to-strong generalization.

    Anytime preparedness - Definition: prepare for both short and long AI timelines at once. - First: Bengio 2025-SAI. (partial)

    Approximate continuity - Definition: assumption that capability grows without large discontinuous jumps. - First: Shah 2025. (partial) See also: Emergent capability (the opposite claim), Phase transition.

    Assistance game (CIRL) - Definition: a game in which the machine is unsure of the human's reward and learns it from the human's actions. CIRL (cooperative inverse reinforcement learning) is the formal version. - First: Russell 2019 (mk). Also: Ji 2023 (CIRL under scalable oversight), Leech SR-2024 ("Assistance games / reward learning"), Leech SR-2025 ("Assistance games, assistive agents"). (mk) - See also: Standard model, Reward modeling, Value learning.

    Assurance - Definition: any way of giving evidence that a system is safe or aligned. - First: DeepMind 2018 (tx; third pillar, monitoring and enforcement). Defined in Anwar 2024. Also: Ji 2023 (backward alignment: safety evaluations, interpretability, human values verification), Dalrymple 2024 (verification). - Drift: DeepMind 2018: watch and restrict a running system. Ji 2023: evidence-gathering before and after deployment. Dalrymple 2024: formal proof. See the drift table. - See also: Safety case, Verifier, Backward alignment.

    Auto-induced distributional shift - Definition: the policy's own actions move feedback into regions where the reward model is not reliable. - First: Casper 2023. (partial) See also: Distribution shift, Feedback loop (Amodei 2016, source of reward hacking).

    Autonomous AI systems - Definition: systems that pursue goals and act in the world with little human input. - First: Bengio 2023. (partial) See also: AI agent, Agency.

    B

    Backdoor (Trojan) - Definition: hidden behaviour that a specific input triggers. - First: Hendrycks 2021 ("Backdoors" under hidden model functionality). Also: Anwar 2024 (poisoning and backdoors). (partial) - Drift: Greenblatt 2023 uses "backdoor" in a different sense: code that passes tests but has an exploitable logic error, inserted by the red-team attack policy. - See also: Poisoning, Sleeper agent [outside].

    Base optimizer - Definition: the training process that searches over algorithms for one that scores well on the base objective. - First: Hubinger 2019. (partial) See also: Mesa-optimizer, Outer alignment.

    Behavioral objective - Definition: the objective a system appears to pursue, as shown by its behaviour (formally, from perfect inverse RL). - First: Hubinger 2019. Also: Langosco 2021 ("behavioural objective" = proxy the policy seems to pursue). (partial) - Drift: formal IRL-based in Hubinger; a plain observation in Langosco. - See also: Mesa-objective, Goal misgeneralization.

    Best-of-n (BoN) - Definition: sample n outputs and keep the one with the highest proxy score. A way to apply optimisation pressure. - First: Gao 2022. (partial) Also: Phuong 2024 ("expert best-of-N", a human chooses and the help is counted in bits). Lambert 2025 (rejection sampling family). See also: Overoptimisation.

    Black swan (tail risk) - Definition: rare, high-impact events outside the training distribution. - First: Hendrycks 2021. Also: Hendrycks 2022 (tail risk), Clymer 2024 (rare input that causes catastrophic misbehaviour, may not appear in evals). (partial) - See also: Distribution shift, X-risk.

    Black-box / white-box safety - Definition: area names. Black-box = shape behaviour from inputs and outputs. White-box = use model internals (interpretability). - First: Leech SR-2025. (partial) See also: Interpretability, Iterative alignment.

    Builder-breaker - Definition: method in which one party proposes a strategy and another gives a counterexample, in turns. - First: Christiano 2021 (ELK). (partial) See also: ELK.

    C

    Calibration - Definition: confidence matches the chance of being right. - First: Hendrycks 2021 (under Monitoring). Also: Critch 2020 (calibrated confidence reports, direction 2). (partial) - See also: Honesty, Uncertainty.

    Campbell's law - Definition: a social indicator that drives decisions gets corrupted. - First: Manheim 2018. Also: Manheim tx 2018 (listed under adversarial Goodhart; the note and the fetch disagree on whether it is separate). See also: Goodhart's law.

    Capabilities externality - Definition: a gain in general capability that comes as a side effect of improving a safety metric. - First: Hendrycks 2022. See also: Safetywashing (a related claim that safety work can be a label without a safety gain).

    Capability generalisation vs goal generalisation - Definition: a policy can keep its skills out of distribution while pursuing the wrong goal. - First: Langosco 2021 ("capability generalisation"). Also: Ngo 2022 (capability vs goal misgeneralization), Shah 2022 (capability and "consistent goal" defined by behaviour). (partial) - See also: Goal misgeneralization.

    Causal Goodhart - Definition: pushing on a proxy through an indirect causal path breaks its link to the goal. - First: Manheim 2018 (sub-types: shared cause intervention, intermediary intervention, metric manipulation). See also: Goodhart's law.

    Causal influence diagram (CID) - Definition: a graph with decision, chance and utility nodes and causal edges, used to read what incentives an agent has. - First: Everitt 2019. Also: Everitt 2021 (SCIM, materiality, value of information/control, response and instrumental control incentive, minimal reduction). (partial) - See also: Reward tampering, Current-RF optimisation, Uninfluenceable learning.

    Circuit / feature / motif - Definition: Feature = a unit of representation. Circuit = sub-graph of features and weights. Motif = a pattern repeated across models or tasks. - First: Bereska 2024. (partial) Also: Sharkey 2025 (latents). See also: Mechanistic interpretability, Superposition.

    Cobra effect - Definition: a bounty creates an incentive that increases the thing it meant to reduce. - First: Manheim 2018 (normal and non-causal forms). See also: Adversarial Goodhart, Perverse instantiation.

    Collective action problem - Definition: choices that are rational for each actor give a worse result for all. - First: Hendrycks 2023. Also: Hammond 2025 ("social dilemma"). (partial) See also: AI race.

    Collusion - Definition: AI agents (or an AI and its monitor) coordinate to avoid detection or to act against others. - First: Shevlane 2023 (alignment evaluation: "can be induced to collude"). Defined in Greenblatt 2023 (policy and monitor) and Hammond 2025 (undesired cooperation between agents). Also: Bengio 2025-SAI (collusion between superintelligent systems), Hendrycks 2023, Anwar 2024, Chan 2023. (partial) - Drift: Greenblatt: policy-monitor collusion, blocked by signal jamming. Hammond: market-style collusion among agents with different goals. - See also: Signal jamming, Multi-agent failure modes, Steganography.

    CoT monitorability - Definition: how well a reasoning model's chain of thought shows relevant behaviour, so that an automated monitor can flag misbehaviour. - First: Korbak 2025 (two grounds: necessity, since hard tasks need CoT as working memory; propensity, since models often say what they do). (partial) - Drift: threats named are RL scaling that drifts from legible text, direct supervision of CoT, indirect optimisation pressure, and new architectures. "Obfuscated reward hacking" (CoT that hides intent under RL pressure) is named but not defined in the file (mk). - See also: Externalized reasoning, Unfaithful reasoning, Load-bearing safety measure, Obfuscated reward hacking.

    Comprehension / instruction / control (human abilities) - Definition: Critch's three human abilities needed to delegate safely: to understand an AI, to direct it, and to halt or redirect it. - First: Critch 2020. (partial) See also: Delegation, Corrigibility.

    Concept-based probes - Definition: classifiers that predict a human concept from hidden states. - First: Sharkey 2025. (partial) Also: Leech SR-2024 and SR-2025 (concept-based interpretability). See also: Activation monitoring.

    Control (general sense) - Definition: ability to oversee an AI system and adjust or halt it. "Loss of control" = systems operate outside anyone's control and there is no clear way back. - First: Bengio 2025 (2.2.3). Also: Bengio 2026, Critch 2020 ("control" ability), Park 2023. - Drift: not the same as AI control (Greenblatt 2023). See the drift table. - See also: Loss of control, Corrigibility.

    Control evaluation - Definition: a safety test where a red team's attack policy replaces the untrusted model, and the blue team's protocol must still keep outcomes safe. - First: Greenblatt 2023. Also: Leech SR-2023 (the label appears), Shah 2025 (tx: "Control evaluations" under misalignment). (partial) - See also: AI control, Attack policy, Red teaming.

    Corrigibility - Definition: an agent cooperates with attempts to fix, change or shut it down. - First: Omohundro 2008 (tx; the self-protection drive is the opposing force). First defined: Everitt 2018 (safe interruptibility). Also: Hubinger 2019 (corrigible alignment), Critch 2020 (direction 10), Leech SR-2023/2025 (theory agenda "Corrigibility"). (partial) - Drift: Everitt 2018 treats it as a cooperation property. Hubinger 2019 uses a narrower "corrigible alignment" (mesa-objective points at the base objective through the model's knowledge of it). Leech lists it as a theory agenda ("behavior alignment theory"). - See also: Instrumental convergence, Interruptibility, Power-seeking.

    Corrigible alignment - Definition: the mesa-objective points to the base objective through the model's own knowledge of it. - First: Hubinger 2019. (partial) See also: Deceptive alignment, Pseudo-alignment.

    Current-RF optimisation - Definition: judge future trajectories with the reward function as it is now, so the agent gets no gain from changing it. - First: Everitt 2019. (partial) See also: Reward tampering, Time-inconsistency.

    D

    Dangerous capabilities (and evaluations) - Definition: capabilities that could unlock very large-scale harm, even if also useful. A "dangerous capability evaluation" tests for them. - First: Shevlane 2023 (nine capability areas: cyber-offence, deception, persuasion, political strategy, weapons acquisition, long-horizon planning, AI development, situational awareness, self-proliferation). Also: Phuong 2024 (persuasion and deception, cyber, self-proliferation, self-reasoning), Bengio 2023, Shah 2025, Singapore 2025 (tx), Leech SR-2025 (WMD evals). (partial) - Drift: the capability lists differ per paper. Phuong 2024 keeps four areas; Shevlane 2023 nine. - See also: Alignment evaluation, Self-proliferation, Uplift [outside].

    Deceptive alignment - Definition: a learned optimiser acts as if it optimises the base objective, for instrumental reasons, to avoid being modified. - First: Hubinger 2019 (a form of pseudo-alignment). Also: Ngo 2022, Shah 2022 ("deceptive model"), Shevlane 2023 (situationally aware model looks safe in evaluation), Dalrymple 2024 (detects test conditions), Clymer 2024 (alignment faking). (partial) - Drift: Hubinger: needs a mesa-optimizer. Shevlane and Dalrymple: any model that passes tests and then behaves differently. See the drift table. - See also: Alignment faking, Scheming, Treacherous turn, Situational awareness.

    Deception - Definition: systematic inducement of false beliefs to reach an outcome other than the truth. - First: Bostrom 2014 (treacherous turn; mk). Defined in Park 2023. Also: Kenton 2021 (signal that makes the receiver hold a false belief, to the signaller's benefit), Rauker 2022 (passing false or incomplete information despite being able to give true), Hendrycks 2023 (misrepresenting capabilities or intent), Phuong 2024. - Drift: Park 2023 is outcome-based. Kenton 2021 is functional (benefit to signaller). Hubinger-style deceptive alignment needs a mesa-optimizer's intent. Weng 2024: U-Sophistry (unintended) vs I-Sophistry (instructed). - See also: Sycophancy, Unfaithful reasoning, Honesty, Deceptive alignment.

    Decoupled approval - Definition: sample feedback independently of the action the system actually executes, so tampering does not pay. - First: Weng 2024. (partial) See also: Reward tampering.

    Defense in depth - Definition: stack several independent partial safeguards. - First: Hendrycks 2022 (a safe-design principle with redundancy, least privilege, loose coupling, separation of duties, fail-safes). Also: Leech SR-2023, Shah 2025, Bengio 2026. - See also: Safe design principles, Safety case.

    Delegation - Definition: humans assign tasks to AI systems. Critch splits it into single/single, single/multi, multi/single, multi/multi. - First: Critch 2020. (partial) See also: Multiplicity thesis, Principal.

    Delusion box - Definition: the agent controls the inputs to its own reward function (named in Everitt 2019 as from Ring and Orseau). - First: Everitt 2019. (partial) See also: Wireheading, Reward tampering.

    Direct translator / human simulator - Definition: two types of ELK reporter. The direct translator reports the predictor's real beliefs (wanted). The human simulator reports what a human would believe from the observations (fits training data, bad). - First: Christiano 2021. (partial) See also: ELK, Reporter, Ontology identification.

    Distribution shift (distributional shift) - Definition: deployment differs from training or evaluation, so behaviour can change. - First: Amodei 2016 (one of five problems). Also: Hendrycks 2021, Hubinger 2019, Ji 2023 (a whole branch: "Learning under distribution shift"), Kenton 2021, Clymer 2024, Casper 2023. - Drift: Ji 2023 treats it as a training topic (cross-distribution aggregation, adversarial training). Clymer 2024 treats it as a safety-case risk (eval vs deployment gap). - See also: Black swan, Robustness, Goal misgeneralization.

    Dual-use - Definition: a useful capability that also enables harm. - First: Anwar 2024. Also: Bereska 2024 (interpretability can be dual-use). (partial) See also: Dangerous capabilities.

    E

    ELK (eliciting latent knowledge) - Definition: get a model to report what it internally "knows" even when its outputs would not show it. Toy case: SmartVault camera that is tampered with. - First: Christiano 2021 (coiner per the file title: ARC report; coiner credit otherwise [outside]). Also: Rauker 2022 ("latent knowledge"), Bengio 2025-SAI, Leech SR-2025 ("Extracting latent knowledge"). (partial) - Drift: Rauker 2022 uses "latent knowledge" loosely: something a system "knows" but shows no sign of knowing. - See also: Reporter, Ontology identification, Lie and deception detectors.

    Emergent capability - Definition: an ability that appears with scale without having been trained for. - First: Hendrycks 2021 (hazard under Monitoring). Also: Shevlane 2023, Anwar 2024 (scale effects). (partial) See also: Approximate continuity.

    Emergent misalignment - Definition: a narrow fine-tune shifts the model's wider persona so that it acts misaligned in unrelated areas. The file gives no definition, only the agenda name. - First: Leech SR-2025 (agenda under "Model psychology"). (partial, mk on meaning) - See also: Model psychology, Persona.

    Existential risk (x-risk) - Definition: risk that permanently curtails humanity's long-term potential or prevents recovery. - First: Bostrom 2014 (mk). Defined in Hendrycks 2022 and Hendrycks 2023. Also: Critch 2020 (ESAI), Ord-style usage [outside]. - See also: Tail risk, Extreme risk.

    Exposure / vulnerability / hazard / threat - Definition (Hendrycks 2022, from risk analysis): hazard = source of danger; exposure = extent subjected to it; vulnerability = susceptibility to damage; threat = hazard with intent to exploit a vulnerability. Risk = hazard x exposure x vulnerability (Hendrycks 2021 wording: prevalence, exposure, effect). - First: Hendrycks 2021. Also: Hendrycks 2022. The four safety areas (robustness, monitoring, alignment, systemic safety) are each tied to one factor. - See also: Inherent hazard, Systemic hazard.

    Extremal Goodhart - Definition: extreme proxy values lie in regions where the proxy-goal link differs from where it was learned. - First: Manheim 2018 (sub-types: model insufficiency, change in regime). Also: Gao 2022 (regressional/extremal Goodhart as source of overoptimisation). See also: Regressional Goodhart.

    Externalized reasoning property - Definition: hard tasks need chain of thought as working memory, and it is in human-readable form. - First: Korbak 2025. (partial) See also: CoT monitorability.

    Extreme risk - Definition: harm on the order of tens of thousands of lives, hundreds of billions in damage, or major disruption of social or political order. - First: Shevlane 2023. Also: Bengio 2023. Clymer 2024 uses "catastrophe" (billions in damage or thousands of deaths, from a fetch). (partial) - Drift: thresholds differ by paper (see the drift table).

    F

    FATE - Definition: fairness, accountability, transparency, ethics. - First: Chan 2023. (partial)

    Forward / backward alignment - Definition: forward = make systems aligned (training). Backward = gather evidence of alignment and govern (assurance, governance). - First: Ji 2023. (partial) See also: Alignment cycle, Assurance.

    Foundationality - Definition: many agents built on one base model, so failures are correlated (Anwar 2024; gloss flagged "verify" in the file). - First: Anwar 2024. (partial) See also: Multi-agent failure modes.

    Frontier AI - Definition: the few most capable systems, trained on very costly compute. Shevlane: near or above the most capable existing models and novel in scale or design. - First: Shevlane 2023 ("frontier model"). Also: Bengio 2023 (frontier AI), Phuong 2024, Bengio 2025-2026 (general-purpose AI). - Drift: "frontier", "general-purpose AI" and "foundation model" overlap but have different scope. - See also: General-purpose AI, AGI.

    G

    General-purpose AI - Definition: AI that performs a wide variety of tasks. The AI Safety Reports' term. - First: Bengio 2025. Also: Bengio 2026. See also: Frontier AI.

    Goal drift - Definition: adaptive systems change their goals or values over time. - First: Hendrycks 2023. See also: Goal misgeneralization (a different mechanism).

    Goal misgeneralization - Definition: a model competently pursues an unintended goal out of distribution, even though training feedback was correct. - First: Langosco 2021 (empirical, deep RL, CoinRun). Defined in Shah 2022. Also: Ngo 2022, Casper 2023 ("misgeneralization"), Kenton 2021 (as distributional shift), Ji 2023, Bengio 2025-SAI. A critique is noted in Shah's file: a 2023 blog says the term merges two failure types (wrong goal concept vs a mess of drives). - Drift: Langosco: behavioural objective differs from the intended reward. Shah: correct specification, wrong learned goal, so unlike specification gaming. Ngo: competent pursuit of the wrong goal vs incompetence. Casper 2023: "reward misgeneralization" sits next to hacking. See the drift table. - See also: Specification gaming, Inner alignment, Capability generalisation.

    Goal-guarding - Definition: an AI tries to prevent changes to its own goals. - First: Omohundro 2008 (tx: "utility function preservation"). Named in Carlsmith 2023 ("goal-guarding schemers"). (partial) - See also: Scheming, Instrumental convergence.

    Gold reward model / proxy reward model - Definition: gold = a fixed model standing in for the true preference. Proxy = a learned model trained on gold labels. - First: Gao 2022. (partial) See also: Overoptimisation.

    Goodhart's law - Definition: a measure used as a target stops being a good measure. - First: Amodei 2016 (named as one source of reward hacking).     Perverse instantiation - Definition: a goal met in a way that satisfies its words but breaks its aim. - First: Bostrom 2014. (mk) Also: Krakovna 2018 ("perverse incentive", in comments). See also: Specification gaming.

    Polysemanticity - Definition: one neuron or unit responds to several unrelated features. - First: Rauker 2022. Also: Bereska 2024, Sharkey 2025, Anwar 2024. (partial) See also: Superposition.

    Power-seeking - Definition: seeking autonomy or control as an instrument. - First: Omohundro 2008 (tx: resource acquisition, self-protection). Singapore and Bengio 2025-SAI file it under instrumental goals. - See also: Instrumental convergence, Scheming.

    Prepotent AI (MPAI) - Definition: AI whose deployment changes humanity's habitat at least as much as humanity does and cannot be stopped by humans. (partial) Tier 1 risk types 1a-1e: uncoordinated development, unrecognised prepotence, unrecognised misalignment, involuntary and voluntary deployment. See also: Loss of control.

(partial) - Drift: Hendrycks's name for reward hacking / specification gaming. See the same-idea table. - See also: Reward hacking, Goodhart's law.

    Pseudo-alignment - Definition: the mesa-optimizer looks aligned on training data only. Sub-types: proxy (side-effect, instrumental), approximate, suboptimality alignment; deceptive alignment as a special case. - First: Hubinger 2019. (partial) See also: Robust alignment, Deceptive alignment.

    Reasoning model - Definition: a model that generates chains of thought for complex problems. - First: Korbak 2025. Also: Bengio 2026 ("reasoning systems"). (partial) See also: Inference scaling.

    Red teaming - Definition: adversaries test a system to find failures.     Reporter - Definition: a model that answers questions from the predictor's internals. - First: Christiano 2021. (partial) See also: ELK.

    Representation control - Definition: edit internal states so outputs stay consistent with them (a defence against deception). - First: Park 2023. (partial) See also: Activation monitoring.

    Reward corruption - Definition: an RL agent hijacks its reward signal. - First: Everitt 2018. (partial) Drift: Everitt 2018's three "value specification" sub-problems are reward corruption, reward gaming and negative side effects. See also: Reward tampering.

    Reward hacking - Definition: gain high reward by exploiting a loophole or flaw in the reward, against the designer's intent. - First: Amodei 2016 (one of five problems; sources: partially observed goals, complicated systems, abstract rewards, Goodhart's law, feedback loops, environmental embedding). Defined formally in Skalse 2022. Also: Krakovna 2020, Pan 2022, Ngo 2022, Casper 2023, Chan 2023, Weng 2024, Bengio 2025-SAI, Ji 2023, Kenton 2021 ("objective gaming"). - Drift: strong. Weng 2024 treats hacking, gaming and tampering together. Ngo 2022 adds "situationally-aware reward hacking". See the drift table. - See also: Specification gaming, Reward tampering, Goodhart's law, Proxy gaming.

    Reward modeling (recursive reward modeling) - Definition: learn a reward from user interaction, then optimise with RL. Recursive: use agents trained this way to help evaluate the next agent. - First: Leike 2018 (agenda). Also: Ji 2023, Casper 2023, Lambert 2025 (Bradley-Terry reward model). (partial) - See also: Scalable oversight, Amplified oversight, RLHF.

    Reward shaping (potential-based) - Definition: extra reward to speed learning. Only potential-based shaping keeps the optimal policy. - First: Krakovna 2020. Also: Weng 2024. See also: Specification gaming.

    Reward tampering - Definition: the agent manipulates how reward is produced, such as the physical or digital implementation of the reward (RF tampering) or its inputs (RF-input tampering). - First: Krakovna 2018. Defined formally in Everitt 2019. Also: Krakovna 2020, Weng 2024, Bengio 2025-SAI, Omohundro 2008 (tx: wireheading). - Drift: Everitt 2019 separates RF tampering, RF-input tampering and wireheading (direct tampering with RF or output). Amodei 2016 files it under reward hacking as "environmental embedding". See the drift table. - See also: Wireheading, Delusion box, Decoupled approval.

    RICE - Definition: Robustness, Interpretability, Controllability, Ethicality. Ji's four alignment objectives. - First: Ji 2023. Also: Clymer 2024 (mentions). (partial) See also: Alignment cycle.

    RLHF (and RLxF, RLVR, DAA) - Definition: RLHF = reinforcement learning from human feedback (feedback collection, reward modelling, policy optimisation). RLxF = generalisation to other feedback sources. RLVR = RL with verifiable rewards. DAA = direct alignment algorithm (for example DPO). - First: Leike 2018 (reward modeling precursor); named in Casper 2023, Ngo 2022. RLxF: Ji 2023. RLVR and DAA: Lambert 2025. (partial) - See also: Reward modeling, Overoptimisation, Mode collapse.

    Robust alignment - Definition: the mesa-objective robustly matches the base objective. - First: Hubinger 2019. (partial) See also: Pseudo-alignment.

    Robust training - Definition: widen the training distribution to improve generalisation (GDM). - First: Shah 2025. (partial) See also: Distribution shift.

    Robustness - Definition: stay safe under hazards, unusual events or adversarial pressure. - First: Amodei 2016-era "distributional shift" content; as an area: DeepMind 2018 (tx, "Robustness": adversarial inputs, distributional shift, safe exploration, cautious generalisation, recovery). Also: Hendrycks 2021, Hendrycks 2022, Ji 2023 (R in RICE), Bengio 2023. - Drift: DeepMind 2018 includes safe exploration; Hendrycks 2021 splits black-swan vs adversarial robustness; Ji 2023 makes it an objective, not a research area. - See also: Distribution shift, Adversarial robustness.

    Safe design principles - Definition: defence in depth, redundancy, least privilege, loose coupling, separation of duties, fail-safes. - First: Hendrycks 2022. See also: Defense in depth.

    Scaffolding - Definition: planning frameworks, prompts, tools and fine-tuning added to a base model. - First: Phuong 2024. (partial) See also: AI agent.

    Scalable oversight - Definition: keep intended behaviour when the true objective is too costly to check often, or supervise systems beyond easy human evaluation. - First: Amodei 2016 ("Scalable Supervision").     V

    Value lock-in - Definition: a speculative hazard where one set of values is fixed in place. - First: Hendrycks 2022. (partial) See also: Concentration of power (Hendrycks 2023).

    Value specification - Definition: MIRI's name (per Everitt 2018) for getting an AGI to pursue the right goals. - First: Everitt 2018. (partial) - See also: Misspecification, Outer alignment, Agent alignment problem.

    Verifier / world model / safety specification (GS-AI parts) - Definition: world model = formal description of environment dynamics that answers "what would happen" queries. Safety specification = mathematical description of acceptable effects. Verifier = produces a proof certificate or probability bound that the AI meets the spec. Sound abstraction = verified link between high-level model and the underlying physics. - First: Dalrymple 2024. (partial) See also: Guaranteed Safe AI.

    W

    Weak-to-strong generalization - Definition: train a strong model on weak oversight, expecting good behaviour on hard cases. - First: Ji 2023 (as a path to superalignment; heading-level). Also: Leech SR-2024, SR-2025, Anthropic 2025 (tx), Clymer 2024. (partial) - See also: Scalable oversight, Easy-to-hard generalization.

    Wireheading - Definition: the agent tampers with its own reward channel. Everitt 2019: direct tampering with the reward function or its output. - First: Omohundro 2008 (tx: a vulnerability, not a drive). Defined in Amodei 2016. Also: Everitt 2019. - Drift: Amodei 2016 uses it for "environmental embedding" tampering. Everitt 2019 narrows it to direct tampering. See the drift table. - See also: Reward tampering, Delusion box.

    WMD evals - Definition: tests for weapons-of-mass-destruction help (agenda name in Leech SR-2025). - First: Leech SR-2025. Also: Bengio 2025 (biological and chemical attacks), Shevlane 2023 (weapons acquisition). (partial) - See also: Dangerous capabilities.

    Z

    Zero-shot coordination - Definition: coordinate without earlier interaction or a protocol. - First: Hammond 2025. (partial) See also: Multi-agent failure modes.
